\begin{abstract}
Decentralized multi-robot task allocation in dynamic, contested environments is vulnerable to adversarial corridor blockages and inconsistent subjective beliefs. Standard market mechanisms such as the Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA) assume homogeneous environmental estimates and trigger costly fleet-wide re-auctions whenever scheduled paths are invalidated. We propose \textbf{Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA)}, a decentralized market coordination mechanism that couples type-aware expected recourse cost valuation over discrete contingency trees with an analytical \textbf{Pinsker claim margin} and a \textbf{belief-consistent consensus hysteresis deadband}. By piggybacking compact belief vectors onto consensus exchanges and deriving an exact analytical Total Variation (TV) drift bound, the Pinsker claim margin provably guarantees that when the bounded belief-divergence condition ($D_{\mathrm{TV}} \le \eta_{\mathrm{eff}}$, Assumption~1) holds, simultaneous Phase-1 safe-claim conflicts are zero (\aeTheoremConflicts\ conflicts across \aeSafeClaimsAdhered\ adhered claims; \aeConfidenceLevel\% Clopper--Pearson exact upper bound \aeTheoremConflictRateUpper\%). Across all \aeSafeClaimsTotal\ unconditional Phase-1 safe claims, ACA achieves \aeAdherencePerClaim\% empirical adherence (\aeAdherencePerInstance\% per instance) and reduces Phase-1 safe-claim conflicts by \aeHfourSafeConflictReductionPct\% (from \aeNoMarginSafeConflicts\ down to \aeTotalSafeConflicts, achieving \aeConflictFreeRunPct\% conflict-free runs). In Phase~2 consensus, the hysteresis deadband strictly eliminates cyclic outbidding limit cycles induced by divergent beliefs, guaranteeing finite-time auction stabilization. When corridor blockages occur, precomputed local contingency branch pivots require zero re-auction messages and reduce total auction message volume by \aeMsgDiffCbba\ messages relative to standard CBBA ($\aeAcaMsgs \pm \aeAcaMsgsCI$ vs.\ $\aeCbbaMsgs \pm \aeCbbaMsgsCI$, $p < \aeAlphaSignificance$). We evaluate ACA across five graph topology families against thirteen literature baselines and ablations across multi-regime parameter sweeps ($\aeTotalTrials$ total discrete trials) and in closed-loop Software-in-the-Loop (SITL) simulations in PyBullet ($N = \aeSitlMatchedTrials$ trials across \aeSitlPhysicalInstances\ physical instances $\times$ \aeSitlMethodsCount\ methods) with active ArUco vision perception and Reciprocal Velocity Obstacles (RVO) collision avoidance. Across $N = \aeMatchedBenchmarkInstances$ matched benchmark instances across five graph topologies, ACA demonstrated significant coordination gains over standard CBBA (\aeCompletionDiffCbba~pp), CBBA+LR (\aeHtwoCbbaLrDiff~pp, $p < \aePvalCbbaLr$), and SSI+LR (\aeHtwoSsiLrDiff~pp), matches CBBA-PR+LR in completion while significantly reducing conflicts and messages, and in continuous SITL physics achieved the highest net welfare among decentralized auction mechanisms ($\aeSitlAcaWelfare \pm \aeSitlAcaWelfareCI$, outperforming ACA No Margin $\aeSitlNoMarginWelfare \pm \aeSitlNoMarginWelfareCI$, CBBA-PR+LR $\aeSitlCbbaPrLrWelfare \pm \aeSitlCbbaPrLrWelfareCI$, and Implicit Coordination $\aeSitlImplicitWelfare \pm \aeSitlImplicitWelfareCI$, all $p < \aeAlphaSignificance$, while non-auction single-path reactive CTP Replan achieves $\aeSitlCtpWelfare \pm \aeSitlCtpWelfareCI$), while reducing task conflicts by \aeSitlConflictReductionNoMarginPct\%--\aeSitlConflictReductionCbbaPrLrPct\% relative to No Margin and CBBA-PR+LR ($p < \aeAlphaSignificance$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} multi-robot systems operating in contested, dynamic domains must allocate, sequence, and execute spatial tasks under environmental uncertainty and adversarial disruption~\cite{choi2009cbba, wilde2022online, korsah2013comprehensive, gerkey2004formal, dias2006market, nunes2017taxonomy}. Examples include search-and-rescue through damaged infrastructure, parcel transport in high-density automated warehouses, and perimeter reconnaissance under active interdiction~\cite{agmon2011multi, bopardikar2014dynamic, sujit2008unmanned}. In these settings, decentralized auction mechanisms---particularly the Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA)~\cite{choi2009cbba} and Sequential Single-Item (SSI) auctions~\cite{koenig2006ssi}---are widely adopted due to polynomial communication complexity and submodular efficiency guarantees.

However, existing decentralized auction mechanisms rely on two fragile assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Global Re-Auction Overhead:} When a robot encounters an unexpected corridor blockage, its single-path plan is invalidated. Standard CBBA drops the entire task bundle and initiates fleet-wide market re-auctions, inducing high message volume and coordination latency~\cite{buckman2019partial, otte2020auctions}.
    \item \textbf{Belief Consistency Assumption:} When communication is range-limited or packet drops occur, robots observe disparate local events and maintain diverging subjective beliefs. Because market bidding rules assume aligned information, inconsistent beliefs cause robots to submit conflicting winning claims on identical tasks, inducing cyclic outbidding churn, redundant travel, and coordination failures~\cite{kundu2024multi, alighanbari2005implicit, tsiogkas2018decentralized, ghaffarkhah2011communication}.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges without continuous, bandwidth-heavy belief synchronization, this paper introduces \textbf{Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA)}. Rather than applying ad-hoc bid penalties, ACA couples discrete contingency recourse trees with an information-theoretic \textbf{claim margin} derived from Pinsker's inequality and total variation bounds.

The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Analytical TV Drift Bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:tv_drift}):} Deriving an exact analytical upper bound on Total Variation belief drift under piggybacked belief vectors and bounded transition likelihood ratios, satisfying bounded estimation error ($\mathrm{TV} \le \eta_{\text{eff}}$) by construction without heuristic parameter tuning.
    \item \textbf{Type-Aware Expected Recourse Valuation (Proposition~\ref{prop:recourse_dmg}):} A tractable formulation for computing expected traversal costs of depth-$d$ contingency recourse trees under Bayesian adversary posteriors, satisfying Diminishing Marginal Gains (DMG) for polynomial-time bundle construction.
    \item \textbf{Tight Path-Contrast Lipschitz Continuity (Proposition~\ref{prop:tight_lipschitz}):} Establishing a tight path-contrast Lipschitz constant $L_{r,j} = \sum_{k=1}^K |c(u_0 \to u_k) + c(D_k) - c(P_j)| \Delta\pi(e_k)$ on the belief simplex $\Delta(\Theta)$ via the telescoping Total Variation identity for product Bernoulli measures.
    \item \textbf{Conflict-Freedom and Belief-Update Invariance (Theorem~\ref{thm:conflict_freedom}):} A closed-form Pinsker claim margin $m_{r, j}$ that bridges subjective valuations across true beliefs, provably guaranteeing both zero simultaneous Phase-1 claims and no post-claim preference reversal upon belief synchronization.
    \item \textbf{Finite-Time Consensus Stabilization (Theorem~\ref{thm:stabilization}):} Proving that standard CBBA can enter cyclic outbidding limit cycles under divergent beliefs, and that ACA's belief-consistent consensus hysteresis deadband strictly eliminates limit cycles, guaranteeing stabilization in $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{T}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}_{\text{comm}}))$ rounds.
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Recourse Message Reduction (Proposition~\ref{prop:message_reduction}):} Establishing exponential reduction $\mathcal{O}(\bar{\pi}^d |\mathcal{R}| \mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}_{\text{comm}}))$ in blockage-induced re-auction message complexity via precomputed recourse branches.
    \item \textbf{Unified Benchmark and Closed-Loop Physics SITL:} Extensive empirical evaluation across $N = \aeMatchedBenchmarkInstances$ matched instances across five graph topologies and thirteen literature baselines and ablations, coupled with closed-loop continuous PyBullet physics SITL ($N = \aeSitlMatchedTrials$) with active ArUco vision perception and RVO collision avoidance where ACA achieves the highest net welfare ($\aeSitlAcaWelfare \pm \aeSitlAcaWelfareCI$) among auction methods while reducing task conflicts by \aeSitlConflictReductionNoMarginPct\%--\aeSitlConflictReductionCbbaPrLrPct\% relative to No-Margin and CBBA-PR+LR ($p < \aeAlphaSignificance$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Market-Based Task Allocation}
Decentralized auctions provide polynomial approximations for multi-robot task allocation~\cite{choi2009cbba, koenig2006ssi, bai2023group, johnson2012asynchronous, tovey2005geometry, luo2014provably, williams2017decentralized}. Choi et al.~\cite{choi2009cbba} introduced CBBA, guaranteeing \aeCbbaEfficiencyBound\% submodular efficiency under consensus. Buckman et al.~\cite{buckman2019partial} developed CBBA with partial replanning (CBBA-PR) for dynamic arrivals, which we adapt to corridor blockages. Ponda et al.~\cite{ponda2012cc} and Bandyopadhyay et al.~\cite{bandyopadhyay2017risk} introduced chance-constrained (CC-CBBA) and risk-aware formulations to bound route failure risk. However, these methods assume homogeneous world models; when observations diverge, standard consensus rules fail to prevent conflicting claims.

\subsection{Coordination under Inconsistent World Views}
Planning under belief inconsistency was pioneered by Alighanbari and How~\cite{alighanbari2005implicit}, where implicit task allocation suffers frequent conflicts under divergent worldviews. Kundu et al.~\cite{kundu2024multi} enforced action-consistency constraints during joint continuous trajectory optimization. In contrast, ACA addresses discrete combinatorial task bundle allocation in decentralized market auctions. By deriving a closed-form Pinsker margin, ACA provably prevents conflicting discrete task assignments under bounded belief estimation error without communicating continuous trajectory plans or high-dimensional belief vectors~\cite{ghadimi2014multi, olfatisaber2007consensus, albrecht2018reasoning}.

\subsection{Contingency Routing and Adversarial Interdiction}
Routing under stochastic edge blockages is classical in the Canadian Traveller Problem (CTP)~\cite{papadimitriou1991shortest, eyerich2010high, nikolova2008route} and vehicle routing with recourse~\cite{bertsimas1996new, wood1993deterministic, agmon2011multi}. Peters et al.~\cite{peters2024contingency} formulated contingency games for continuous trajectory planning. In contrast, ACA integrates discrete contingency recourse trees directly into decentralized auction valuations under Bayesian adversary inference, executing local detours with zero communication overhead upon blockage. Table~\ref{tab:related_works} outlines feature differentiation against prior literature.

\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{Environment and Persistent Blockage Process}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a known, connected undirected graph where $V$ represents physical waypoints and task locations, and $E$ represents traversable corridors with nominal weights $w_e > 0$. A designated subset of edges $E_{\text{risk}} \subseteq E$ are susceptible to persistent blockage.

We model each risky edge $e \in E_{\text{risk}}$ as a two-state Markov chain $X_e^t \in \{\aeStateOpen, \aeStateBlocked\}$ (\aeStateOpen\ = Open, \aeStateBlocked\ = Blocked):
\begin{align}
    P(X_e^{t+1} = 1 \mid X_e^t = 0, \theta, s_t) &= h_\theta(e, s_t) \quad \text{(Onset Hazard)} \\
    P(X_e^{t+1} = 0 \mid X_e^t = 1) &= \mu \quad \text{(Clearance Rate)}
\end{align}
A global budget $k$ bounds simultaneously blocked edges: $\sum_{e \in E_{\text{risk}}} X_e^t \le k$.

\subsection{Adversary Behavioral Type Space and Vulnerability Partition}
To capture realistic structural threats, the risky edge set $E_{\text{risk}}$ is partitioned into three disjoint topological vulnerability classes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $E_{\text{risk}}^{(1)}$ (\textbf{Stochastic Clutter, $\theta_1$}): High-frequency, low-detour-penalty peripheral and aisle edges with stationary independent onset hazard $h_{\theta_1}(e) = p_0$.
    \item $E_{\text{risk}}^{(2)}$ (\textbf{Reactive Traffic Interdiction, $\theta_2$}): Multi-robot collector corridors where onset hazard scales with local robot traffic density: $h_{\theta_2}(e) = \min(\aeMaxProb, p_0 + \alpha N_e(t))$, where $N_e(t)$ denotes robots adjacent to edge $e$.
    \item $E_{\text{risk}}^{(3)}$ (\textbf{Strategic Cut Interdiction, $\theta_3$}): High-betweenness topological bridge and cut edges whose blockage isolates graph partitions unless tasks are proactively allocated across the cut ($p_{\text{cut}} = \aeParamPCut$).
    \item \textbf{Cyclic Patrol ($\theta_4$, Out-of-Model)}: Cycles blockages sequentially along ordered topological bottlenecks for misspecification robustness testing ($\theta_4 \notin \Theta$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Robot Fleet and Communication Model}
A fleet $\mathcal{R} = \{1, \dots, R\}$ of robots services tasks $\mathcal{T} = \{1, \dots, M\}$. Each task $j \in \mathcal{T}$ has destination $v_j \in V$, reward $R_j$, arrival step $a_j$, and deadline $D_j$. Robots communicate locally within metric disk range $r_{\text{comm}} = \aeCommRange\text{ m}$ subject to stochastic packet drop probability $p_{\text{drop}} = \aePacketDropProb$ (with regime sweeps across $p_{\text{drop}} \in \{0.02, 0.10, 0.15, 0.30\}$).

\section{The ACA Method}

\subsection{Bayesian Type Filter with Piggybacked Beliefs}
Each robot $r$ maintains a subjective posterior belief distribution $B_r^t(\theta)$ over $\Theta = \{\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3\}$. Upon observing edge $e$ at tick $t$ transitioning from state $X_e^t$ to $X_e^{t+1}$, robot $r$ updates its belief via transition likelihoods:
\begin{equation}
    B_r^{t+1}(\theta) \propto B_r^t(\theta) \prod_{e \in \mathcal{O}_r^t} P(X_e^{t+1} \mid X_e^t, \theta, s_t)
\end{equation}
The subjective edge blockage marginal is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \pi_r(e) = \sum_{\theta \in \Theta} B_r(\theta) P(X_e = 1 \mid \theta, s_t)
\end{equation}
For edges outside $E_{\text{risk}}$, $\pi_r(e) = 0$. Each robot piggybacks its compact belief vector $B_r^t \in \Delta^{|\Theta|-1}$ (only two floating-point scalars for $|\Theta|=\aeAdversaryCount$) onto every outgoing consensus message at zero additional packet overhead.

\subsection{Expected-Recourse Valuation and Active Tree Pruning}
To evaluate task $j$, robot $r$ identifies a primary path $P_j = (u_0, u_1, \dots, u_m)$ that minimizes risk-weighted traversal cost $w_{\text{eff}}(e) = w_e(1 + \lambda_{\text{risk}} \pi_r(e))$. Let $(u_k, v_k)_{k=1}^K$ be the oriented risky edges along $P_j$, and let $D_k$ denote precomputed detour paths branching from node $u_k$ that avoid $(u_k, v_k)$. Branches with low failure probability ($\pi_r(e_k) < \tau_{\text{prune}}$) are actively pruned to bound contingency tree depth while preserving recourse coverage.

Robot $r$'s bid equals its expected recourse cost under the independent marginal model:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}_r[C_j] &= \sum_{k=1}^K \left[ \prod_{i < k} (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] \pi_r(e_k) \notag \\
    &\quad\quad \times \Big( c(u_0 \to u_k) + c(D_k) \Big) \notag \\
    &\quad + \left[ \prod_{i=1}^K (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] c(P_j)
\label{eq:expected_recourse_cost}
\end{align}

\subsection{Two-Phase Pinsker Stabilization}
Each robot $r$ tracks estimated peer beliefs $\hat{B}_s^{(r)}$ for all $s \ne r$ based on the last-received piggybacked consensus message with communication staleness $\Delta t_{r,s}$. Let $D'_{\text{KL}} = \max_{s \ne r} D_{\text{KL}}(B_r \parallel \hat{B}_s^{(r)})$. Under Lemma~\ref{lem:tv_drift}, Total Variation drift is bounded by $\eta_{\text{eff}}(\Delta t_{r,s})$.

\noindent\textbf{Phase 1 (Fast-Track Safe Commitment):} To determine whether task $j$ can be safely committed locally without multi-round consensus, robot $r$ evaluates the \textbf{Pinsker Claim Margin}:
\begin{equation}
    m_{r, j} = L_{r, j} \left( \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}} \right) + \max_{s \ne r} L_{s, j} \eta_{\text{eff}} + \delta_{\text{map}}
\label{eq:pinsker_margin}
\end{equation}
where $L_{r, j}$ is the tight path-contrast Lipschitz constant (Proposition~\ref{prop:tight_lipschitz}). Robot $r$ commits task $j$ into its bundle during Phase~1 without consensus negotiation if and only if:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} < \min_{s \ne r} \hat{c}_s^r(j)
\label{eq:claim_rule}
\end{equation}
where peer costs $\hat{c}_s^r(j)$ are evaluated under ToM beliefs $\hat{B}_s^{(r)}$.

\noindent\textbf{Phase 2 (Belief-Consistent Consensus Hysteresis):} Tasks not committed in Phase~1 are evaluated in Phase~2 consensus rounds. In consensus resolution (Action Y1/Y2), when robot $s$ compares its bid against incumbent winner $r$'s bid for task $j$, robot $s$ only revokes or outbids $r$ if:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{c}_s^s(j) + \kappa_{\text{hyst}} m_{r,s,j} < \hat{c}_r^r(j)
\label{eq:consensus_hysteresis}
\end{equation}
Within the belief-ambiguity deadband $|\hat{c}_s^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j)| \le \kappa_{\text{hyst}} m_{r,s,j}$, the bid difference is statistically indistinguishable from belief divergence $\mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s)$; robots preserve the incumbent winner, strictly preventing cyclic outbidding churn (Theorem~\ref{thm:stabilization}).

\subsection{Autonomous Recourse Execution}
During traversal, if robot $r$ observes that its approaching edge $(u, v)$ is blocked, it activates its precomputed contingency detour branch $D_k$ locally with \textbf{zero communication messages}. A partial re-auction is triggered if and only if all precomputed branches up to depth $d$ are blocked or the remaining path cost exceeds deadline constraints.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded Estimation Error]
$\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ for all $s \ne r$.
\label{ass:tom}
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Analytical Total Variation Drift Bound]
Let each robot $r$ append its compact belief vector $B_r^t \in \Delta^{|\Theta|-1}$ to every outgoing consensus message. Suppose peer $s$ transmits its belief vector at tick $t - \Delta t_{r,s}$, and robot $r$ maintains estimated belief $\hat{B}_s^{(r),t} = B_s^{t - \Delta t_{r,s}}$. If observation transition likelihood ratios are bounded by $\Lambda_{\max} = \max_{e \in E_{\text{risk}}, x \in \{0,1\}, \theta, \theta' \in \Theta} \frac{P(X_e = x \mid \theta)}{P(X_e = x \mid \theta')} < \infty$ with local observation rate $\gamma_{\text{obs}}$, then the Total Variation drift over $\Delta t_{r,s}$ elapsed communication intervals satisfies:
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{TV}\left(B_s^t, \hat{B}_s^{(r),t}\right) &\le \eta_{\text{eff}}(\Delta t_{r,s}) \equiv \min\Big(1.0,\; \eta_0 \notag \\
    &\quad\quad + \tanh\left(\tfrac{1}{2} \gamma_{\text{obs}} \Delta t_{r,s} \log \Lambda_{\max}\right)\Big)
\label{eq:analytical_tv_drift}
\end{align}
Consequently, Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} ($\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) \le \eta_{\text{eff}}$) is guaranteed analytically by construction without heuristic parameter tuning.
\label{lem:tv_drift}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Bayes' rule, the posterior log-odds between any pair of adversary types $\theta, \theta' \in \Theta$ after $\Delta t$ steps with $n_{\text{obs}} \le \gamma_{\text{obs}} \Delta t$ local observations satisfies:
\begin{align}
    \left| \log \frac{B_s^t(\theta)}{B_s^t(\theta')} - \log \frac{B_s^{t-\Delta t}(\theta)}{B_s^{t-\Delta t}(\theta')} \right| &\le \sum_{k=1}^{n_{\text{obs}}} \left| \log \frac{P(X_k \mid \theta)}{P(X_k \mid \theta')} \right| \notag \\
    &\le \gamma_{\text{obs}} \Delta t \log \Lambda_{\max}.
\end{align}
By Le Cam's inequality relating relative entropy and Total Variation distance under bounded likelihood ratios, the maximal TV contraction on the probability simplex satisfies $\mathrm{TV}(P, Q) \le \tanh(\frac{1}{4} \max_{\theta,\theta'} |\log (P(\theta)Q(\theta') / P(\theta')Q(\theta))|)$. Setting initial observation uncertainty to $\eta_0$ and applying the log-odds upper bound yields Eq.~(\ref{eq:analytical_tv_drift}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Expected Recourse Cost and Diminishing Marginal Gains]
Let $T_r(j)$ be a depth-$d$ contingency recourse tree constructed for task $j$ with primary path $P_j = (u_0, \dots, u_m)$, risky edges $(e_1, \dots, e_K)$, and contingency detours $D_k$ branching from $u_k$. Under subjective edge marginals $\pi_r(e) = \sum_{\theta} B_r(\theta) P(X_e = 1 \mid \theta)$, the expected traversal cost is given by:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}_r[C_j] &= \sum_{k=1}^K \left[ \prod_{i < k} (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] \pi_r(e_k) \notag \\
    &\quad\quad \times \Big( c(u_0 \to u_k) + c(D_k) \Big) \notag \\
    &\quad + \left[ \prod_{i=1}^K (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] c(P_j).
\label{eq:recourse_cost_prop}
\end{align}
Furthermore, defining the marginal score for inserting task $j$ at position $k$ in bundle $b_r$ with task reward $R_j$ as:
\begin{equation}
    S_r(j \mid b_r) = \max\!\Big(0,\; R_j - \big(\mathbb{E}_r[C(b_r \oplus_k j)] - \mathbb{E}_r[C(b_r)]\big)\Big)
\label{eq:bundle_dmg_score}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{E}_r[C(b_r \oplus_k j)] - \mathbb{E}_r[C(b_r)]$ is the marginal expected recourse cost of insertion, satisfies Diminishing Marginal Gains (DMG): for any bundles $b \subseteq b'$ and task $j \notin b'$, $S_r(j \mid b) \ge S_r(j \mid b')$, ensuring polynomial-time bundle construction and submodular auction approximation guarantees~\cite{choi2009cbba}.
\label{prop:recourse_dmg}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Eq.~(\ref{eq:recourse_cost_prop}) follows directly from the law of total expectation over the mutually exclusive first-blockage index partition along primary path $P_j$. The DMG property follows from the submodularity of marginal insertion recourse costs across metric graphs under non-decreasing detour penalties.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Tight Path-Contrast Lipschitz Continuity]
For any task $j$ with primary path $P_j$, risky edges $(e_1, \dots, e_K)$, and detour branches $D_k$, the expected recourse bid $c_r(j \mid B)$ is $L_{r, j}$-Lipschitz continuous with respect to the Total Variation metric on belief simplex $\Delta(\Theta)$:
\begin{equation}
    |c_r(j \mid B) - c_r(j \mid B')| \leq L_{r, j} \cdot \mathrm{TV}(B, B')
\label{eq:tight_lipschitz}
\end{equation}
where the tight path-contrast Lipschitz constant is given by:
\begin{equation}
    L_{r, j} = \sum_{k=1}^K \Big| c(u_0 \to u_k) + c(D_k) - c(P_j) \Big| \cdot \Delta\pi(e_k)
\label{eq:path_contrast_L}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta\pi(e_k) = \max_{\theta \in \Theta} \pi(e_k \mid \theta) - \min_{\theta \in \Theta} \pi(e_k \mid \theta)$.
\label{prop:tight_lipschitz}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Delta C_k = c(u_0 \to u_k) + c(D_k) - c(P_j)$ denote the recourse cost difference between taking detour $D_k$ versus completing primary path $P_j$. From Eq.~(\ref{eq:recourse_cost_prop}), subtracting the constant primary cost $c(P_j)$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    c_r(j \mid B) - c(P_j) = \sum_{k=1}^K \left[ \prod_{i < k} (1 - \pi(e_i \mid B)) \right] \pi(e_k \mid B) \Delta C_k.
\end{equation}
By the telescoping product identity for product Bernoulli measures, for any sequence of probabilities $p_i, q_i \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    \left| \prod_{i=1}^k (1 - p_i) - \prod_{i=1}^k (1 - q_i) \right| \le \sum_{i=1}^k |p_i - q_i|.
\end{equation}
Because $\pi(e \mid B) = \sum_{\theta} B(\theta) P(X_e = 1 \mid \theta)$ is linear in $B$, $|\pi(e \mid B) - \pi(e \mid B')| \le \Delta\pi(e) \mathrm{TV}(B, B')$. Applying the triangle inequality across the mutually exclusive detour branch events:
\begin{align}
    |c_r(j \mid B) &- c_r(j \mid B')| \notag \\
    &\le \sum_{k=1}^K |\Delta C_k| \cdot |\pi(e_k \mid B) - \pi(e_k \mid B')| \notag \\
    &\le \left( \sum_{k=1}^K |\Delta C_k| \Delta\pi(e_k) \right) \mathrm{TV}(B, B'),
\end{align}
establishing Eq.~(\ref{eq:tight_lipschitz}) with tight path-contrast constant $L_{r, j}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conflict-Freedom and Belief-Update Invariance of Phase-1 Claims]
Let robot $r$ evaluate task $j$ under safe claim condition $\hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} < \min_{s \ne r} \hat{c}_s^r(j)$, where $m_{r, j} = L_{r, j} (\sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}}) + \max_{s \ne r} L_{s, j} \eta_{\text{eff}} + \delta_{\text{map}}$. Under Lemma~\ref{lem:tv_drift} and bounded map discrepancies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conflict-Freedom:} No peer robot $s \ne r$ can satisfy the Phase-1 safe-claim rule for task $j$ in the same allocation round.
    \item \textbf{Belief-Update Invariance:} For any posterior belief $B \in \{B_r, B_s\}$, robot $r$'s true expected recourse cost strictly dominates peer $s$'s valuation: $c_r(j \mid B) < c_s(j \mid B)$. Consequently, when robots $r$ and $s$ subsequently synchronize beliefs, no preference reversal can occur.
\end{enumerate}
\label{thm:conflict_freedom}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Part 1 (Conflict-Freedom): Suppose for contradiction that peer $s$ also claims task $j$ in Phase~1, so $\hat{c}_s^s(j) + m_{s, j} < \hat{c}_r^s(j)$. Summing the inequalities:
\begin{equation}
    m_{r, j} + m_{s, j} < \left( \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j) \right) + \left( \hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j) \right).
\label{eq:thm1_sum}
\end{equation}
By the triangle inequality of Total Variation: $\mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) \le \mathrm{TV}(B_r, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) + \mathrm{TV}(\hat{B}_s^{(r)}, B_s) \le \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D_{\text{KL}}(B_r \parallel \hat{B}_s^{(r)})} + \eta_{\text{eff}}$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:tight_lipschitz}, $|\hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j)| \le L_{s, j} \mathrm{TV}(\hat{B}_s^{(r)}, B_s) \le L_{s, j} \eta_{\text{eff}}$, and $|\hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j)| \le L_{r, j} \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) + \delta_{\text{map}} \le L_{r, j}(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}}) + \delta_{\text{map}}$. Summing these bounds gives:
\begin{equation}
    \left( \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j) \right) + \left( \hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j) \right) \le m_{r, j} + m_{s, j},
\end{equation}
contradicting Eq.~(\ref{eq:thm1_sum}). Hence simultaneous Phase-1 claims cannot occur.

Part 2 (Belief-Update Invariance): Suppose robot $r$ claims task $j$ in Phase 1, so $\hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} < \hat{c}_s^r(j)$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:tight_lipschitz} and Lemma~\ref{lem:tv_drift}, peer $s$'s cost under its true belief $B_s$ satisfies $c_s(j \mid B_s) \ge \hat{c}_s^r(j) - L_{s, j}\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) \ge \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \max_{q \ne r} L_{q, j}\eta_{\text{eff}}$. Meanwhile, robot $r$'s cost evaluated at $B_s$ satisfies $c_r(j \mid B_s) \le \hat{c}_r^r(j) + L_{r, j}\mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) + \delta_{\text{map}} \le \hat{c}_r^r(j) + L_{r, j}(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}}) + \delta_{\text{map}}$. Combining these two inequalities with Eq.~(\ref{eq:pinsker_margin})--(\ref{eq:claim_rule}) yields $c_r(j \mid B_s) \le \hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} - \max_{q \ne r} L_{q, j}\eta_{\text{eff}} < \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \max_{q \ne r} L_{q, j}\eta_{\text{eff}} \le c_s(j \mid B_s)$. Thus, even when beliefs synchronize to $B_s$, robot $r$'s true cost strictly dominates peer $s$'s valuation, preventing preference reversal.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Time Consensus Stabilization and Oscillation-Freedom]
Consider Phase~2 consensus for task $j$ under divergent subjective beliefs $B_r \ne B_s$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Limit Cycle in Standard CBBA:} Standard CBBA without margin can enter a cyclic outbidding limit cycle whenever the true cost difference satisfies $0 < |c_r(j \mid B_r) - c_s(j \mid B_s)| < L_j \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s)$.
    \item \textbf{Stabilization under Hysteresis Deadband:} Equipping Phase~2 consensus with the belief hysteresis deadband $\kappa_{\text{hyst}} m_{r,s,j}$ (where robot $s$ outbids incumbent $r$ only if $\hat{c}_s^s(j) + \kappa_{\text{hyst}} m_{r,s,j} < \hat{c}_r^r(j)$) strictly eliminates outbidding limit cycles, guaranteeing that the decentralized auction reaches a quiescent consensus state in at most $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{T}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}_{\text{comm}}))$ communication rounds.
\end{enumerate}
\label{thm:stabilization}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Part 1 (Limit Cycle): Suppose $c_r(j \mid B_r) < c_s(j \mid B_r)$, but due to belief divergence, $c_s(j \mid B_s) < c_r(j \mid B_s)$. When robot $r$ claims task $j$ with bid $c_r(j \mid B_r)$, message propagation to $s$ occurs over delay $\Delta t$. When $s$ evaluates its own bid $c_s(j \mid B_s)$ against received bid $c_r(j \mid B_r)$, if $c_s(j \mid B_s) < c_r(j \mid B_r)$, $s$ outbids $r$ with a newer timestamp. Upon receiving $s$'s bid, robot $r$ re-evaluates under its local belief $B_r$, finding $c_r(j \mid B_r) < c_s(j \mid B_s)$, and outbids $s$ again. This creates an infinite bid-churn oscillation of period $2 \Delta t$.

Part 2 (Hysteresis Stabilization): Under ACA Phase~2 consensus, let $r$ be the incumbent winner of task $j$ with cost $\hat{c}_r^r(j)$. Robot $s$ can only revoke/outbid $r$ if $\hat{c}_s^s(j) + \kappa_{\text{hyst}} m_{r,s,j} < \hat{c}_r^r(j)$. Within the ambiguity deadband $|\hat{c}_s^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j)| \le \kappa_{\text{hyst}} m_{r,s,j}$, Proposition~\ref{prop:tight_lipschitz} establishes that the bid disparity is statistically indistinguishable from belief divergence $\mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s)$; hence, robots preserve the incumbent winner and deterministic tie-breaking applies. Outside the deadband, outbidding requires a strict cost reduction exceeding $\kappa_{\text{hyst}} m_{r,s,j} > 0$. Because task costs are bounded below by zero and each successful outbidding strictly decreases the winning cost by at least $\kappa_{\text{hyst}} m_{\min} > 0$, winning bids form a strictly monotonically decreasing sequence on a compact domain. Across $|\mathcal{T}|$ tasks over a network of diameter $\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}_{\text{comm}})$, outbidding events must terminate in finite steps, bounding consensus convergence to $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{T}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}_{\text{comm}}))$ communication rounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exponential Re-Auction Message Reduction]
Let $\bar{\pi} = \max_{e \in E_{\text{risk}}} \pi(e) < 1$ denote the maximum edge blockage probability. Under a depth-$d$ contingency policy, a robot resolves corridor blockages locally with zero communication messages as long as at least one precomputed recourse branch remains traversable. The expected message overhead for handling task blockages satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[\text{Msgs}_{\text{ACA}}] \le \sum_{j \in \mathcal{T}} \bar{\pi}^{d+1} \mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{R}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}_{\text{comm}})),
\end{equation}
achieving an exponential reduction by factor $\mathcal{O}(\bar{\pi}^d)$ relative to standard CBBA re-auction overhead $\sum_{j \in \mathcal{T}} \bar{\pi} \mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{R}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}_{\text{comm}}))$.
\label{prop:message_reduction}
\end{proposition}

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
(1)~\textit{Hardware Deployment:} Physical multi-robot testbed deployments remain planned future work. (2)~\textit{Prolonged Communication Blackouts:} Under extended network partitions ($\Delta t_{r,s} \gg 1$), the analytical drift bound $\eta_{\text{eff}}(\Delta t_{r,s}) \to 1$ inflates the Pinsker margin $m_{r,j}$, conservatively deferring Phase-1 fast-track claims to Phase-2 consensus. (3)~\textit{Contingency Scalability:} Discrete recourse trees scale with depth $d$; practical deployments employ depth pruning ($d \in \{\aeDepthOne, \aeDepthTwo\}$).

\section{Conclusion}
We presented Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA) for decentralized multi-robot task allocation under strategic corridor blockages and belief inconsistency. By coupling type-aware expected recourse cost valuation with a closed-form Pinsker claim margin, a consensus hysteresis deadband, and autonomous local detours, ACA guarantees zero Phase-1 safe-claim conflicts when $D_{\mathrm{TV}} \le \eta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (\aeTheoremConflicts\ conflicts across \aeSafeClaimsAdhered\ adhered claims) and eliminates outbidding limit cycles without continuous belief synchronization. Across $N = \aeMatchedBenchmarkInstances$ matched benchmark instances across five graph topologies and closed-loop PyBullet SITL simulations ($N = \aeSitlMatchedTrials$), ACA demonstrated significant coordination gains over CBBA (\aeCompletionDiffCbba~pp), CBBA+LR (\aeHtwoCbbaLrDiff~pp, $p < \aePvalCbbaLr$), and SSI+LR (\aeHtwoSsiLrDiff~pp), reduced unconditional safe-claim conflicts by \aeHfourSafeConflictReductionPct\% (achieving \aeConflictFreeRunPct\% conflict-free runs), and in continuous physics achieved the highest net welfare among decentralized auction mechanisms ($\aeSitlAcaWelfare \pm \aeSitlAcaWelfareCI$, outperforming ACA No Margin $\aeSitlNoMarginWelfare \pm \aeSitlNoMarginWelfareCI$, CBBA-PR+LR $\aeSitlCbbaPrLrWelfare \pm \aeSitlCbbaPrLrWelfareCI$, and Implicit Coordination $\aeSitlImplicitWelfare \pm \aeSitlImplicitWelfareCI$, all $p < \aeAlphaSignificance$, while reactive baseline CTP Replan achieves $\aeSitlCtpWelfare \pm \aeSitlCtpWelfareCI$) while reducing task conflicts by \aeSitlConflictReductionNoMarginPct\%--\aeSitlConflictReductionCbbaPrLrPct\% ($p < \aeAlphaSignificance$).
