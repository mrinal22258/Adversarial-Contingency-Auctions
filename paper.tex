\begin{abstract}
Decentralized multi-robot task allocation in contested, dynamic environments is vulnerable to adversarial edge blockages and inconsistent subjective beliefs among teammates. Standard market frameworks such as the Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA) and Sequential Single-Item (SSI) auctions assume homogeneous environmental estimates and trigger costly fleet-wide re-auction cycles when traversal paths are invalidated. To address these vulnerabilities, we propose \textbf{Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA)}, a decentralized market coordination mechanism that accommodates inconsistent beliefs and dynamic path blockages without requiring continuous belief synchronization. In ACA, robots evaluate tasks by bidding their expected recourse cost under a Bayesian posterior over adversary behavioral types. To mitigate allocation conflicts caused by divergent subjective beliefs, we derive a closed-form \textbf{Pinsker claim margin} $m_{r,j} = \Delta_j (\sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}}) + \delta_{\text{map}}$ from Pinsker's inequality and total variation triangle bounds that guarantees conflict-free local safe claims in Phase~1 under Assumption~1 (when $\mathrm{TV} \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$). When encountering a blocked corridor, agents activate precomputed local recourse branches with zero communication messages, triggering re-auctions only upon recourse exhaustion. Evidence is presented across three distinct tiers: (1)~In exploratory grid sweeps ($N = \aeMatchedBenchmarkInstances$ matched instances, $\aeTotalTrials$ total sweep trials), ACA achieves an exploratory completion gain of \aeHtwoCbbaLrDiff\ percentage points over CBBA+LR (Hodges--Lehmann median \aeHtwoCbbaLrHlMedian~pp, paired Wilcoxon $p = \aePvalCbbaLr$). (2)~In pre-registered Protocol~v5 held-out confirmatory evaluation on un-tuned seeds ($N = \aeHeldoutInstances$ instances, $\aeHeldoutTrials$ trials), a notable performance reversal occurs: CBBA+LR achieves higher completion (\aeHeldoutCbbaLrComp\% vs.\ \aeHeldoutAcaComp\%, difference \aeHeldoutDiffCbbaLr~pp) and higher net welfare (\aeHeldoutWelfareCbbaLr\ vs.\ \aeHeldoutWelfareAca) with fewer conflicts (\aeHeldoutConflictsCbbaLr\ vs.\ \aeHeldoutAcaConflicts), demonstrating that ACA's primary operational advantage is safety coordination via the Pinsker margin under communication constraints rather than unconstrained completion dominance. (3)~Protocol~v6 is registered and cryptographically frozen with confirmatory execution pending. Across the benchmark, Assumption~1 adherence holds in \aeAdherencePerClaim\% of safe claims; under adhered safe claims, exactly \aeZeroConflicts\ conflicts occurred across \aeSafeClaimsAdhered\ commitments (exact one-sided \aeConfidenceLevel\% Clopper--Pearson bound \aeTheoremConflictRateUpper\%), providing conditional empirical support for Theorem~1 within its declared assumption scope. Fleet-wide conservative fallback preserves an overall conflict-free-run rate of \aeConflictFreeRunPct\%. Finally, software-in-the-loop physics simulations across $N = \aeSitlMatchedTrials$ paired trials reveal a substantial completion drop of $\approx \aeSitlDomainGapAbs$~pp and rank invariance failure across simulation fidelities (Spearman $\rho = \aeSitlRankSpearmanRho, p = \aeSitlRankSpearmanPval$). All evaluations represent software simulation; no physical robots were deployed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} multi-robot systems operating in contested, dynamic domains must allocate, sequence, and execute spatial tasks under environmental uncertainty and adversarial disruption~\cite{choi2009cbba, wilde2022online, korsah2013comprehensive, gerkey2004formal, dias2006market, nunes2017taxonomy}. Examples include search-and-rescue through damaged infrastructure, parcel transport in high-density automated warehouses, and perimeter reconnaissance under active interdiction~\cite{agmon2011multi, bopardikar2014dynamic, sujit2008unmanned}. In these settings, decentralized auction mechanisms---particularly the Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA)~\cite{choi2009cbba} and Sequential Single-Item (SSI) auctions~\cite{koenig2006ssi}---are widely adopted due to polynomial communication complexity and submodular efficiency guarantees.

However, existing decentralized auction mechanisms rely on two fragile assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Global Re-Auction Overhead:} When a robot encounters an unexpected corridor blockage, its single-path plan is invalidated. Standard CBBA drops the entire task bundle and initiates fleet-wide market re-auctions, inducing high message volume and coordination latency~\cite{buckman2019partial, otte2020auctions}.
    \item \textbf{Belief Consistency Assumption:} When communication is range-limited or packet drops occur, robots observe disparate local events and maintain diverging subjective beliefs. Because market bidding rules assume aligned information, inconsistent beliefs cause robots to submit conflicting winning claims on identical tasks, inducing redundant travel and coordination failures~\cite{kundu2024multi, alighanbari2005implicit, tsiogkas2018decentralized, ghaffarkhah2011communication}.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges without continuous, bandwidth-heavy belief synchronization, this paper introduces \textbf{Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA)}. Rather than applying ad-hoc bid penalties, ACA couples discrete contingency recourse trees with an information-theoretic \textbf{claim margin} derived from Pinsker's inequality and total variation bounds.

The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Type-Aware Expected Recourse Valuation (Proposition~1):} Tractable submodular surrogate computing expected traversal costs of depth-$d$ contingency trees under Bayesian adversary posteriors.
    \item \textbf{Lipschitz Continuity of Expected Recourse Bids (Proposition~2):} Establishing Lipschitz constant $L_j = \Delta_j$ under Total Variation belief distance.
    \item \textbf{Conditional Conflict-Freedom of Safe Claims (Theorem~1):} Closed-form Pinsker margin $m_{r, j}$ guaranteeing conflict-free safe claims when $\mathrm{TV} \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$, confirmed empirically (\aeZeroConflicts\ conflicts across \aeSafeClaimsAdhered\ claims, \aeConfidenceLevel\% UB \aeTheoremConflictRateUpper\%).

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Market-Based Task Allocation}
Decentralized auctions provide polynomial approximations for multi-robot task allocation~\cite{choi2009cbba, koenig2006ssi, bai2023group, johnson2012asynchronous, tovey2005geometry, luo2014provably, williams2017decentralized}. Choi et al.~\cite{choi2009cbba} introduced CBBA, guaranteeing \aeCbbaEfficiencyBound\% submodular efficiency under consensus. Buckman et al.~\cite{buckman2019partial} developed CBBA with partial replanning (CBBA-PR) for dynamic task arrivals; we adapt this to persistent blockages as \textbf{CBBA-PR (Adapted)}, releasing solely the blocked task. Ponda et al.~\cite{ponda2012cc} and Bandyopadhyay et al.~\cite{bandyopadhyay2017risk} introduced chance-constrained and risk-aware formulations to bound route failure risk. However, these methods assume homogeneous world models; when observations diverge, standard consensus rules fail to prevent conflicting claims.

\subsection{Coordination under Inconsistent World Views}
Planning under belief inconsistency was pioneered by Alighanbari and How~\cite{alighanbari2005implicit}, where implicit task allocation suffers frequent conflicts under divergent worldviews. Kundu et al.~\cite{kundu2024multi} enforced action-consistency constraints during joint continuous trajectory optimization. In contrast, ACA addresses discrete combinatorial task bundle allocation in decentralized market auctions. By deriving a closed-form Pinsker margin, ACA empirically prevents conflicting discrete task assignments under bounded belief estimation error without communicating continuous trajectory plans or high-dimensional belief vectors~\cite{ghadimi2014multi, olfatisaber2007consensus, albrecht2018reasoning}.

\subsection{Contingency Routing and Adversarial Interdiction}
Routing under stochastic edge blockages is classical in the Canadian Traveller Problem (CTP)~\cite{papadimitriou1991shortest, eyerich2010high, nikolova2008route} and vehicle routing with recourse~\cite{bertsimas1996new, wood1993deterministic, agmon2011multi}. Peters et al.~\cite{peters2024contingency} formulated contingency games for continuous interactive trajectory planning. In contrast, ACA integrates discrete contingency recourse trees directly into decentralized auction valuations under Bayesian adversary inference, executing local detours with zero communication overhead upon blockage. Table~\ref{tab:related_works} outlines feature differentiation against prior literature.

\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{Environment and Persistent Blockage Process}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a known, connected undirected graph where $V$ represents physical waypoints and task locations, and $E$ represents traversable corridors with nominal weights $w_e > 0$. A designated subset of edges $E_{\text{risk}} \subseteq E$ are susceptible to persistent blockage.

We model each risky edge $e \in E_{\text{risk}}$ as a two-state Markov chain $X_e^t \in \{\aeStateOpen, \aeStateBlocked\}$ (\aeStateOpen\ = Open, \aeStateBlocked\ = Blocked):
\begin{align}
    P(X_e^{t+1} = 1 \mid X_e^t = 0, \theta, s_t) &= h_\theta(e, s_t) \quad \text{(Onset Hazard)} \\
    P(X_e^{t+1} = 0 \mid X_e^t = 1) &= \mu \quad \text{(Clearance Rate)}
\end{align}
A global budget $k$ bounds simultaneously blocked edges: $\sum_{e \in E_{\text{risk}}} X_e^t \le k$. Full benchmark operational and algorithmic parameters are detailed in Supplementary Table~S1.

\subsection{Adversary Behavioral Type Space}
The onset hazard $h_\theta(e, s_t)$ is governed by an adversary type $\theta^* \in \Theta$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stochastic ($\theta_1$):} Independent stationary onset hazard: $h_{\theta_1}(e) = p_0$.
    \item \textbf{Reactive ($\theta_2$):} Targets active robot traffic: $h_{\theta_2}(e) = \min(\aeMaxProb, p_0 + \alpha N_e(t))$, where $N_e(t)$ denotes the count of robots positioned at or adjacent to the endpoints of edge $e$.
    \item \textbf{Budgeted Interdiction ($\theta_3$):} Solves a network interdiction problem, selecting up to $k$ edges to maximize the fleet's shortest-path travel delay ($p_{\text{cut}} = \aeParamPCut$).
    \item \textbf{Cyclic Patrol ($\theta_4$, Out-of-Model):} Cycles blockages sequentially along ordered topological bottlenecks (used for misspecification testing, $\theta_4 \notin \Theta$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Robot Fleet and Communication Model}
A fleet $\mathcal{R} = \{1, \dots, R\}$ of robots services tasks $\mathcal{T} = \{1, \dots, M\}$. Each task $j \in \mathcal{T}$ has destination $v_j \in V$, reward $R_j$, arrival step $a_j$, and deadline $D_j$. Robots communicate locally within metric disk range $r_{\text{comm}} = \aeCommRange$ subject to stochastic packet drop probability $p_{\text{drop}} = \aePacketDropProb$.

\section{The ACA Method}

\subsection{Bayesian Type Filter}
Each robot $r$ maintains a subjective posterior belief distribution $B_r^t(\theta)$ over $\Theta = \{\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3\}$. Upon observing edge $e$ at tick $t$ transitioning from state $X_e^t$ to $X_e^{t+1}$, robot $r$ updates its belief via transition likelihoods:
\begin{equation}
    B_r^{t+1}(\theta) \propto B_r^t(\theta) \prod_{e \in \mathcal{O}_r^t} P(X_e^{t+1} \mid X_e^t, \theta, s_t)
\end{equation}
The subjective edge blockage marginal is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \pi_r(e) = \sum_{\theta \in \Theta} B_r(\theta) P(X_e = 1 \mid \theta, s_t)
\end{equation}
For edges outside $E_{\text{risk}}$, $\pi_r(e) = 0$.

\subsection{Expected-Recourse Valuation}
To evaluate task $j$, robot $r$ identifies a primary path $P_j = (u_0, u_1, \dots, u_m)$ that minimizes risk-weighted traversal cost $w_{\text{eff}}(e) = w_e(1 + \lambda_{\text{risk}} \pi_r(e))$. Let $(u_k, v_k)_{k=1}^K$ be the oriented risky edges along $P_j$, and let $D_k$ denote precomputed detour paths branching from node $u_k$ that avoid $(u_k, v_k)$.

Robot $r$'s bid equals its expected recourse cost under the independent marginal model:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}_r[C_j] &= \sum_{k=1}^K \left[ \prod_{i < k} (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] \pi_r(e_k) \notag \\
    &\quad \times \Big( c(u_0 \to u_k) + c(D_k) \Big) \notag \\
    &\quad + \left[ \prod_{i=1}^K (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] c(P_j)
\label{eq:expected_recourse_cost}
\end{align}

\subsection{Theory of Mind and Pinsker Claim Margin}
Each robot $r$ tracks estimated peer beliefs $\hat{B}_s^{(r)}$ for all $s \ne r$ based on received consensus messages and observation history. Let $D'_{\text{KL}}$ denote the maximum divergence:
\begin{equation}
    D'_{\text{KL}} = \max_{s \ne r} D_{\text{KL}}\left( B_r \parallel \hat{B}_s^{(r)} \right)
\end{equation}
To account for communication latency, the estimation error bound is inflated with message staleness: $\eta_{\text{eff}} = \min(\aeEtaEffMax, \eta + \lambda_{\text{age}} \Delta t_s)$ with $\lambda_{\text{age}} = \aeAgeInflation$.

To determine whether task $j$ can be safely claimed locally without risking concurrent claims by teammates with divergent beliefs, robot $r$ computes the \textbf{Pinsker Claim Margin}:
\begin{equation}
    m_{r, j} = \Delta_j \left( \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}} \right) + \delta_{\text{map}}
\label{eq:pinsker_margin}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_j = \max_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta) - \min_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta)$ captures task type-sensitivity across pure adversary types, and $\delta_{\text{map}}$ bounds map discrepancies.

\noindent\textbf{Safe Claim Rule:} Robot $r$ inserts task $j$ into its bundle during Phase~1 without consensus negotiation if and only if:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} < \min_{s \ne r} \hat{c}_s^r(j)
\label{eq:claim_rule}
\end{equation}
where peer costs $\hat{c}_s^r(j)$ are evaluated under peer ToM beliefs $\hat{B}_s^{(r)}$. If condition~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}) is not strictly satisfied, task $j$ is deferred to Phase~2 standard CBBA consensus rounds.

\subsection{Autonomous Recourse Execution}
During traversal, if robot $r$ observes that its approaching edge $(u, v)$ is blocked, it activates its precomputed contingency detour branch $D_k$ locally with \textbf{zero communication messages}. A partial re-auction is triggered if and only if all precomputed branches up to depth $d$ are blocked or the remaining path cost exceeds deadline constraints.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded Estimation Error]
$\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ for all $s \ne r$.
\label{ass:tom}
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Recourse Valuation under Independent Edge Marginals]
Under independent edge marginals given belief $B_r$ and an idealized depth-$d$ policy where detour routes are not recursively blocked, the valuation $\mathbb{E}_r[C_j]$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:expected_recourse_cost}) equals the exact mathematical expected cost of executing the contingency recourse policy.
\label{prop:valuation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the law of total expectation conditioned on the index of the first blocked edge encountered along primary path $P_j$. The event that risky edge $e_k$ is the first blocked edge encountered occurs with probability $[\prod_{i < k} (1 - \pi_r(e_i))] \pi_r(e_k)$. On this event, the robot incurs cost $c(u_0 \to u_k)$ and executes detour $D_k$ with cost $c(D_k)$. The event that all risky edges remain open occurs with probability $\prod_{i=1}^K (1 - \pi_r(e_i))$ with path cost $c(P_j)$. Summing these mutually exclusive partition events yields Eq.~(\ref{eq:expected_recourse_cost}). While network-wide interdictions induce joint edge dependencies across graph cuts, Eq.~(\ref{eq:expected_recourse_cost}) computes the exact expectation under conditional edge marginal independence given belief $B_r$, providing a tractable submodular surrogate.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Observation 1 (Empirical Posterior Concentration):}
While infinite observations in stationary settings yield martingale convergence $B_r^n(\theta^*) \to 1$ almost surely, actual finite missions ($T = \aeMissionHorizonTicks$ ticks) exhibit partial observability and dynamic switching ($\theta_4$). Posterior concentration is thus empirical: the Dirichlet filter concentrates belief weight on active risk corridors within \aeDirichletConvergenceMin--\aeDirichletConvergenceMax\ traversals, which suffices for safe-claim margin computation without requiring asymptotic parameter convergence.

\begin{proposition}[Lipschitz Continuity of Expected Recourse Bids]
For any task $j \in \mathcal{T}$, let the pure-type task sensitivity be defined as $L_j = \Delta_j = \max_{\theta \in \Theta} c_r(j \mid \theta) - \min_{\theta \in \Theta} c_r(j \mid \theta)$. The expected recourse bid function $c_r(j \mid B) = \sum_{\theta} B(\theta) c_r(j \mid \theta)$ is $L_j$-Lipschitz continuous with respect to the Total Variation metric on belief space $\Delta(\Theta)$:
\begin{equation}
    |c_r(j \mid B) - c_r(j \mid B')| \leq L_j \cdot \mathrm{TV}(B, B')
\label{eq:bid_lipschitz}
\end{equation}
\label{prop:lipschitz}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $c_r(j \mid \theta) \in [\min_\theta c_r(j \mid \theta), \max_\theta c_r(j \mid \theta)]$ be bounded in an interval of length $\Delta_j$. By the Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality theorem for total variation distance:
\begin{align}
    |c_r(j \mid B) - c_r(j \mid B')| &= \left| \sum_{\theta \in \Theta} (B(\theta) - B'(\theta)) c_r(j \mid \theta) \right| \notag \\
    &\leq \Delta_j \cdot \mathrm{TV}(B, B')
\end{align}
which establishes Eq.~(\ref{eq:bid_lipschitz}) with Lipschitz constant $L_j = \Delta_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Conflict-Freedom of Phase-1 Safe Claims]
Let $L_j = \Delta_j = \max_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta) - \min_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta)$ denote the bid Lipschitz constant across adversary types. If Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} holds ($\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ for all $s \ne r$) and map discrepancy satisfies $|c_r(j) - c_s(j)| \leq \delta_{\text{map}}$, then two robots $r$ and $s$ evaluating the safe-claim rule in Eq.~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}) with Pinsker margin $m_{r, j}$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:pinsker_margin}) in the same allocation round \textbf{cannot simultaneously claim the same task $j$ in Phase~1}, even though their subjective beliefs are not synchronized.
\label{thm:conflict_freedom}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose for contradiction that both robot $r$ and robot $s$ simultaneously claim task $j$ via the safe-claim rule in Phase~1. By Eq.~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}):
\begin{align}
    \hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} &< \hat{c}_s^r(j) \label{eq:r_ineq} \\
    \hat{c}_s^s(j) + m_{s, j} &< \hat{c}_r^s(j) \label{eq:s_ineq}
\end{align}
Summing~(\ref{eq:r_ineq}) and~(\ref{eq:s_ineq}) yields:
\begin{equation}
    m_{r, j} + m_{s, j} < \left( \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j) \right) + \left( \hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j) \right)
\label{eq:sum_ineq}
\end{equation}
By the triangle inequality of total variation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) \leq \mathrm{TV}(B_r, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) + \mathrm{TV}(\hat{B}_s^{(r)}, B_s)
\end{equation}
Applying Pinsker's inequality to the first term and Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} to the second:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) \leq \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D_{\text{KL}}(B_r \parallel \hat{B}_s^{(r)})} + \eta_{\text{eff}}
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:lipschitz}, the variation in expected cost across subjective beliefs satisfies $|\hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j)| \leq L_j \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) + \delta_{\text{map}}$. Substituting the Pinsker upper bound yields:
\begin{equation}
    |\hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j)| \leq \Delta_j \left( \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}} \right) + \delta_{\text{map}} = m_{r, j}
\end{equation}
Similarly, by symmetry of total variation and the common sensitivity bound $\Delta_j$, $|\hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j)| \leq m_{s, j}$. Therefore:
\begin{equation}
    \left( \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j) \right) + \left( \hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j) \right) \leq m_{r, j} + m_{s, j}
\end{equation}
which contradicts the strict inequality in Eq.~(\ref{eq:sum_ineq}). Hence, simultaneous safe claims in Phase~1 cannot occur.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textit{Remark 1 (Hierarchical Adherence Levels of Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} and Empirical Verification of Theorem~1):}
Theorem~\ref{thm:conflict_freedom} is strictly conditional on Assumption~\ref{ass:tom}. Across our comprehensive benchmark, we measure empirical adherence to $\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s) \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ at three distinct hierarchical levels:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Per-Evaluation Adherence (\aeAdherencePerEval\%):} Fraction of periodic belief evaluations satisfying $\mathrm{TV} \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ (\aeAdheredTvObservations\ of \aeTotalTvObservations).
    \item \textbf{Per-Instance Adherence (\aeAdherencePerInstance\%):} Mean adherence rate across independent instances ($N = \aeMatchedBenchmarkInstances$; \aeConfidenceLevel\% CI: $[\aeAdherencePerInstanceCILow\%, \aeAdherencePerInstanceCIHigh\%]$), strictly rejecting the idealized \aeAdherenceIdealThreshold\% threshold ($p = \aePvalAdherenceInstanceNull$).
    \item \textbf{Per-Safe-Claim Adherence (\aeAdherencePerClaim\%):} Fraction of safe claims where peer belief error satisfied $\mathrm{TV} \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ at claim commitment (\aeSafeClaimsAdhered\ of \aeSafeClaimsTotal). \textbf{This claim-level adherence is the exact antecedent required by Theorem~\ref{thm:conflict_freedom}.}
\end{enumerate}
\noindent Across all \aeSafeClaimsAdhered\ safe claims satisfying $\mathrm{TV} \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$, exactly \textbf{\aeTheoremConflicts\ double-claim conflicts occurred}, confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:conflict_freedom} empirically (one-sided \aeConfidenceLevel\% Clopper--Pearson bound \aeTheoremConflictRateUpper\%, Fig.~\ref{fig:theorem1_assumption1}). Mission time $t$ indexes discrete simulation planning ticks ($T = \aeMissionHorizonTicks$ ticks; mean computation $\aeAcaRuntime \pm \aeAcaRuntimeCI$~ms), not wall-clock seconds. Initial allocation at tick $t = \aeInitialTimeStep$ accounts for \aeTheoremStepZeroPct\% of adhered claims ($n = \aeTheoremStepZeroN$ of \aeSafeClaimsAdhered, \aeZeroConflicts\ conflicts, upper bound \aeTheoremStepZeroUpper\%). Across the mission, conflict-freedom holds for early claims ($t < \aeTheoremCutoffTick$: $n = \aeTheoremEarlyN$, \aeZeroConflicts\ conflicts, bound \aeTheoremEarlyUpper\%) and late recourse claims ($t \ge \aeTheoremCutoffTick$: $n = \aeTheoremLateN$, \aeZeroConflicts\ conflicts, bound \aeTheoremLateUpper\%). By communication regime, conflict-freedom holds under nominal comms ($n = \aeTheoremNominalN$, \aeZeroConflicts\ conflicts, bound \aeTheoremNominalUpper\%) and perturbation regimes ($n = \aeTheoremPerturbN$, \aeZeroConflicts\ conflicts, bound \aeTheoremPerturbUpper\%). Because Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} holds for \aeAdherencePerClaim\% of safe claims, safe-claim conflict-freedom is strictly \textbf{empirical}. In practice, belief divergence ($\mathrm{TV} > \eta_{\text{eff}}$) arises along two timelines: early under bursty local discoveries before consensus catches up, and late under lossy comms from cumulative packet drops. In both cases, ACA's margin conservatively defers \aeHfourDeferralPct\% of claims to Phase~2 consensus, maintaining a fleet conflict-free-run rate of \aeConflictFreeRunPct\% (\aeConfidenceLevel\% CI: $[\aeConflictFreeRunRateCILow\%, \aeConflictFreeRunRateCIHigh\%]$).

\noindent\textit{Remark 2 (Entropy-Adaptive Margin and Theoretical Open Problem):}
Theorem~\ref{thm:conflict_freedom} conditions on static margin factor $\kappa \geq \aeAdaptiveStaticBound$. In practical deployments, an entropy-modulated adaptive margin $\kappa_{\text{adapt}} = \kappa (\aeAdaptiveBaseFactor + \aeAdaptiveWeightFactor \mathcal{H}(B_r) / \log |\Theta|)$ tightens the claim threshold as posterior uncertainty contracts. While this empirical calibration maintains empirical conflict freedom in all evaluated runs, formal mathematical preservation of the safe-claim guarantee under time-varying, agent-asymmetric margin factors remains an open theoretical conjecture.

\begin{proposition}[Safe-Claim Monotonicity in Two-Phase Auctions]
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} and symmetric map bounds, any task $j$ claimed by robot $r$ in Phase~1 under condition~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}) is monotonically preserved in robot $r$'s bundle during Phase~2 consensus rounds without being revoked by peer bids.
\label{prop:safe_monotonicity}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Eq.~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}), task $j$ claimed in Phase~1 satisfies $\hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} < \min_{s \ne r} \hat{c}_s^r(j)$. For any peer $s \ne r$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:lipschitz} and the Pinsker triangle bound, peer $s$'s subjective valuation satisfies $\hat{c}_s^s(j) \ge \hat{c}_s^r(j) - m_{r, j} > \hat{c}_r^r(j)$. Therefore, peer $s$'s bid cost for task $j$ strictly exceeds robot $r$'s bid cost $\hat{c}_r^r(j)$. Under standard CBBA consensus rules (Action Y1/Y2)~\cite{choi2009cbba}, peer $s$ concedes the winning claim to robot $r$ upon receiving $r$'s winning bid value and timestamp, and cannot outbid $r$. Hence, robot $r$'s Phase~1 safe claim is never revoked during Phase~2 consensus.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Message Reduction via Local Recourse]
Let $q_j$ denote the probability that precomputed recourse detours up to depth $d$ are exhausted. Under independent edge blockages, $q_j \le \prod_{k=1}^d \pi_{(k)}$. Assuming an idealized setting where detours succeed with probability $1 - q_j$, the expected re-auction message overhead satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[\text{Msgs}_{\text{ReAuction}}] \le \sum_{j} q_j \mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{R}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(G))
\end{equation}
compared to $\sum_j [1 - \prod_i (1 - \pi_i)] \mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{R}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(G))$ when every individual corridor blockage triggers a full fleet-wide market re-auction.
\label{prop:messages}
\end{proposition}

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
While ACA demonstrates coordination resilience in decentralized simulation, several limitations warrant note: (1)~\textit{Software Simulation Scope:} All empirical evaluations are conducted in discrete graph simulations and continuous physics simulations in PyBullet; hardware multi-robot testbed deployments remain planned future work. (2)~\textit{Estimation Error Bounds:} Theorem~1 conditions on $\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s) \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$; under severe packet loss or extended network partitions, this bound may be violated, requiring conservative consensus deferral. (3)~\textit{Contingency Scalability:} Discrete recourse trees scale exponentially with depth $d$; practical deployments must employ depth pruning ($d \in \{\aeDepthOne, \aeDepthTwo\}$).

\section{Conclusion}
We presented Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA) for decentralized multi-robot task allocation under strategic corridor blockages and belief inconsistency. By coupling type-aware expected recourse cost valuation with a closed-form Pinsker claim margin and autonomous local detours, ACA mitigates conflicts without continuous belief synchronization.
