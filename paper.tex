\begin{abstract}
Decentralized multi-robot task allocation in contested, dynamic environments is vulnerable to adversarial edge blockages and inconsistent subjective beliefs among teammates. Standard market frameworks such as the Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA) and Sequential Single-Item (SSI) auctions assume homogeneous environmental estimates and trigger costly fleet-wide re-auction cycles when traversal paths are invalidated. To address these vulnerabilities, we propose \textbf{Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA)}, a decentralized market coordination mechanism that accommodates inconsistent beliefs and dynamic path blockages without requiring continuous belief synchronization. In ACA, robots evaluate tasks by bidding their expected recourse cost under a Bayesian posterior over adversary behavioral types. To mitigate allocation conflicts caused by divergent subjective beliefs, we derive a closed-form \textbf{Pinsker claim margin} $m_{r,j} = \Delta_j (\sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}}) + \delta_{\text{map}}$ from Pinsker's inequality and total variation triangle bounds that guarantees conflict-free local safe claims in Phase~1 under Assumption~1 (when $\mathrm{TV} \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$). When encountering a blocked corridor, agents activate precomputed local recourse branches with zero communication messages, triggering re-auctions only upon recourse exhaustion. Rigorous matched empirical evaluations across 5 benchmark map topologies ($N = \aeMatchedBenchmarkInstances$ matched independent instances, $\aeTotalTrials$ total sweep trials) demonstrate that ACA significantly outperforms standard CBBA by $\aeCompletionDiffCbba$ percentage points in mission completion with $\aeMsgDiffCbba$ fewer messages. Relative to the level-execution baseline CBBA+LR, ACA achieves a significant completion gain of $+1.90$~pp ($p = \aePvalCbbaLr$). Relative to CBBA-PR+LR, ACA achieves statistical parity ($+0.25$~pp, Holm-adjusted $p = \aePvalCbbaPrLr$, Bonferroni $p = \aePvalCbbaPrLrBonf$). In discrete graph simulation, ACA is outperformed in raw completion by reactive single-agent replanning (CTP Replan $-4.86$~pp, Implicit Coordination $-3.56$~pp) because auction consensus defers claims; however, Implicit Coordination suffers severe coordination conflicts ($\aeImplicitTotConf \pm \aeImplicitTotConfCI$), whereas ACA reduces safe-claim conflicts by $\aeHfourSafeConflictReductionPct\%$ relative to ACA-no-margin (reducing conflicts from 3,646 to 32) at a modest $\approx 1.5$~pp completion cost. Across the benchmark, Assumption~1 adherence is evaluated at three hierarchical levels: per periodic evaluation (\aeAdherencePerEval\%), per instance bootstrap mean (\aeAdherencePerInstance\%), and per safe claim commitment (\aeAdherencePerClaim\%, which Theorem~1 directly conditions on). Under claim-level adherence, exactly 0 conflicts occurred across \aeSafeClaimsAdhered\ safe claims (exact 95\% upper bound \aeTheoremConflictRateUpper\%), confirming Theorem~1 empirically. Conservative fallback preserves a fleet-wide conflict-free-run rate of \aeConflictFreeRunPct\%. Finally, software-in-the-loop physics simulations across $N = \aeSitlMatchedTrials$ matched trials validate execution dynamics, demonstrating that physical collisions strongly correlate with recourse detours ($r = \aeSitlContactDetourCorr$). All evaluations represent software simulation; no physical robots were deployed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} multi-robot systems operating in contested, dynamic domains must allocate, sequence, and execute spatial tasks under environmental uncertainty and adversarial disruption~\cite{choi2009cbba, wilde2022online, korsah2013comprehensive}. Examples include search-and-rescue through damaged infrastructure, parcel transport in high-density automated warehouses, and perimeter reconnaissance under active interdiction~\cite{agmon2011multi}. In these settings, decentralized auction mechanisms---particularly the Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA)~\cite{choi2009cbba} and Sequential Single-Item (SSI) auctions~\cite{koenig2006ssi}---are widely adopted due to polynomial communication complexity and submodular efficiency guarantees.

However, existing decentralized auction mechanisms rely on two fragile assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Global Re-Auction Overhead:} When a robot encounters an unexpected corridor blockage, its single-path plan is invalidated. Standard CBBA drops the entire task bundle and initiates fleet-wide market re-auctions, inducing high message volume and coordination latency~\cite{buckman2019partial}.
    \item \textbf{Belief Consistency Assumption:} When communication is range-limited or packet drops occur, robots observe disparate local events and maintain diverging subjective beliefs. Because market bidding rules assume aligned information, inconsistent beliefs cause robots to submit conflicting winning claims on identical tasks, inducing redundant travel and coordination failures~\cite{kundu2024multi, alighanbari2005implicit}.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges without continuous, bandwidth-heavy belief synchronization, this paper introduces \textbf{Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA)}. Rather than applying ad-hoc bid penalties, ACA couples discrete contingency recourse trees with an information-theoretic \textbf{claim margin} derived from Pinsker's inequality and total variation bounds.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Market-Based Task Allocation}
Decentralized auctions provide polynomial approximations for multi-robot task allocation~\cite{choi2009cbba, koenig2006ssi, bai2023group, johnson2012asynchronous}. Choi et al.~\cite{choi2009cbba} introduced CBBA, guaranteeing 50\% submodular efficiency under consensus. Buckman et al.~\cite{buckman2019partial} developed CBBA with partial replanning (CBBA-PR) for dynamic task arrivals; we adapt this to persistent blockages as \textbf{CBBA-PR (Adapted)}, releasing solely the blocked task. Ponda et al.~\cite{ponda2012cc} introduced Chance-Constrained CBBA (CC-CBBA) to bound route failure risk. However, these methods assume homogeneous world models; when observations diverge, standard consensus rules fail to prevent conflicting claims.

\subsection{Coordination under Inconsistent World Views}
Planning under belief inconsistency was pioneered by Alighanbari and How~\cite{alighanbari2005implicit}, where implicit task allocation suffers frequent conflicts under divergent worldviews. Kundu et al.~\cite{kundu2024multi} enforced action-consistency constraints during joint continuous trajectory optimization. In contrast, ACA addresses discrete combinatorial task bundle allocation in decentralized market auctions. By deriving a closed-form Pinsker margin, ACA empirically prevents conflicting discrete task assignments under bounded belief estimation error without communicating continuous trajectory plans or high-dimensional belief vectors~\cite{ghadimi2014multi}.

\subsection{Contingency Routing and Adversarial Interdiction}
Routing under stochastic edge blockages is classical in the Canadian Traveller Problem (CTP)~\cite{papadimitriou1991shortest, eyerich2010high, nikolova2008route} and vehicle routing with recourse~\cite{bertsimas1996new, wood1993deterministic, agmon2011multi}. Peters et al.~\cite{peters2024contingency} formulated contingency games for continuous interactive trajectory planning. In contrast, ACA integrates discrete contingency recourse trees directly into decentralized auction valuations under Bayesian adversary inference, executing local detours with zero communication overhead upon blockage. Table~\ref{tab:related_works} outlines feature differentiation against prior literature.

\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{Environment and Persistent Blockage Process}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a known, connected undirected graph where $V$ represents physical waypoints and task locations, and $E$ represents traversable corridors with nominal weights $w_e > 0$. A designated subset of edges $E_{\text{risk}} \subseteq E$ are susceptible to persistent blockage.

We model each risky edge $e \in E_{\text{risk}}$ as a 2-state Markov chain $X_e^t \in \{0, 1\}$ (0 = Open, 1 = Blocked):
\begin{align}
    P(X_e^{t+1} = 1 \mid X_e^t = 0, \theta, s_t) &= h_\theta(e, s_t) \quad \text{(Onset Hazard)} \\
    P(X_e^{t+1} = 0 \mid X_e^t = 1) &= \mu \quad \text{(Clearance Rate)}
\end{align}
A global budget $k$ bounds simultaneously blocked edges: $\sum_{e \in E_{\text{risk}}} X_e^t \le k$. Table~\ref{tab:system_params} lists operational parameters across all benchmark environments.

\subsection{Adversary Behavioral Type Space}
The onset hazard $h_\theta(e, s_t)$ is governed by an adversary type $\theta^* \in \Theta$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stochastic ($\theta_1$):} Independent stationary onset hazard: $h_{\theta_1}(e) = p_0$.
    \item \textbf{Reactive ($\theta_2$):} Targets active robot traffic: $h_{\theta_2}(e) = \min(1.0, p_0 + \alpha N_e(t))$, where $N_e(t)$ denotes the count of robots positioned at or adjacent to the endpoints of edge $e$.
    \item \textbf{Budgeted Interdiction ($\theta_3$):} Solves a network interdiction problem, selecting up to $k$ edges to maximize the fleet's shortest-path travel delay ($p_{\text{cut}} = 0.85$).
    \item \textbf{Cyclic Patrol ($\theta_4$, Out-of-Model):} Cycles blockages sequentially along ordered topological bottlenecks (used for misspecification testing, $\theta_4 \notin \Theta$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Robot Fleet and Communication Model}
A fleet $\mathcal{R} = \{1, \dots, R\}$ of robots services tasks $\mathcal{T} = \{1, \dots, M\}$. Each task $j \in \mathcal{T}$ has destination $v_j \in V$, reward $R_j$, arrival step $a_j$, and deadline $D_j$. Robots communicate locally within metric disk range $r_{\text{comm}} = 45.0$ subject to stochastic packet drop probability $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.05$.

\section{The ACA Method}

\subsection{Bayesian Type Filter}
Each robot $r$ maintains a subjective posterior belief distribution $B_r^t(\theta)$ over $\Theta = \{\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3\}$. Upon observing edge $e$ at tick $t$ transitioning from state $X_e^t$ to $X_e^{t+1}$, robot $r$ updates its belief via transition likelihoods:
\begin{equation}
    B_r^{t+1}(\theta) \propto B_r^t(\theta) \prod_{e \in \mathcal{O}_r^t} P(X_e^{t+1} \mid X_e^t, \theta, s_t)
\end{equation}
The subjective edge blockage marginal is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \pi_r(e) = \sum_{\theta \in \Theta} B_r(\theta) P(X_e = 1 \mid \theta, s_t)
\end{equation}
For edges outside $E_{\text{risk}}$, $\pi_r(e) = 0$.

\subsection{Expected-Recourse Valuation}
To evaluate task $j$, robot $r$ identifies a primary path $P_j = (u_0, u_1, \dots, u_m)$ that minimizes risk-weighted traversal cost $w_{\text{eff}}(e) = w_e(1 + \lambda_{\text{risk}} \pi_r(e))$. Let $(u_k, v_k)_{k=1}^K$ be the oriented risky edges along $P_j$, and let $D_k$ denote precomputed detour paths branching from node $u_k$ that avoid $(u_k, v_k)$.

Robot $r$'s bid equals its expected recourse cost under the independent marginal model:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}_r[C_j] &= \sum_{k=1}^K \left[ \prod_{i < k} (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] \pi_r(e_k) \notag \\
    &\quad \times \Big( c(u_0 \to u_k) + c(D_k) \Big) \notag \\
    &\quad + \left[ \prod_{i=1}^K (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] c(P_j)
\label{eq:expected_recourse_cost}
\end{align}

\subsection{Theory of Mind and Pinsker Claim Margin}
Each robot $r$ tracks estimated peer beliefs $\hat{B}_s^{(r)}$ for all $s \ne r$ based on received consensus messages and observation history. Let $D'_{\text{KL}}$ denote the maximum divergence:
\begin{equation}
    D'_{\text{KL}} = \max_{s \ne r} D_{\text{KL}}\left( B_r \parallel \hat{B}_s^{(r)} \right)
\end{equation}
To account for communication latency, the estimation error bound is inflated with message staleness: $\eta_{\text{eff}} = \min(0.5, \eta + \lambda_{\text{age}} \Delta t_s)$ with $\lambda_{\text{age}} = 0.005$.

To determine whether task $j$ can be safely claimed locally without risking concurrent claims by teammates with divergent beliefs, robot $r$ computes the \textbf{Pinsker Claim Margin}:
\begin{equation}
    m_{r, j} = \Delta_j \left( \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}} \right) + \delta_{\text{map}}
\label{eq:pinsker_margin}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_j = \max_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta) - \min_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta)$ captures task type-sensitivity across pure adversary types, and $\delta_{\text{map}}$ bounds map discrepancies.

\noindent\textbf{Safe Claim Rule:} Robot $r$ inserts task $j$ into its bundle during Phase~1 without consensus negotiation if and only if:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} < \min_{s \ne r} \hat{c}_s^r(j)
\label{eq:claim_rule}
\end{equation}
where peer costs $\hat{c}_s^r(j)$ are evaluated under peer ToM beliefs $\hat{B}_s^{(r)}$. If condition~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}) is not strictly satisfied, task $j$ is deferred to Phase~2 standard CBBA consensus rounds.

\subsection{Autonomous Recourse Execution}
During traversal, if robot $r$ observes that its approaching edge $(u, v)$ is blocked, it activates its precomputed contingency detour branch $D_k$ locally with \textbf{zero communication messages}. A partial re-auction is triggered if and only if all precomputed branches up to depth $d$ are blocked or the remaining path cost exceeds deadline constraints.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded Estimation Error]
$\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ for all $s \ne r$.
\label{ass:tom}
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Recourse Valuation under Independent Edge Marginals]
Under independent edge marginals given belief $B_r$ and an idealized depth-$d$ policy where detour routes are not recursively blocked, the valuation $\mathbb{E}_r[C_j]$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:expected_recourse_cost}) equals the exact mathematical expected cost of executing the contingency recourse policy.
\label{prop:valuation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the law of total expectation conditioned on the index of the first blocked edge encountered along primary path $P_j$. The event that risky edge $e_k$ is the first blocked edge encountered occurs with probability $[\prod_{i < k} (1 - \pi_r(e_i))] \pi_r(e_k)$. On this event, the robot incurs cost $c(u_0 \to u_k)$ and executes detour $D_k$ with cost $c(D_k)$. The event that all risky edges remain open occurs with probability $\prod_{i=1}^K (1 - \pi_r(e_i))$ with path cost $c(P_j)$. Summing these mutually exclusive partition events yields Eq.~(\ref{eq:expected_recourse_cost}). While network-wide interdictions induce joint edge dependencies across graph cuts, Eq.~(\ref{eq:expected_recourse_cost}) computes the exact expectation under conditional edge marginal independence given belief $B_r$, providing a tractable submodular surrogate.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Observation 1 (Empirical Posterior Concentration vs. Finite-Horizon Realities):}
Under an idealized stationary environment with distinguishable type likelihoods ($D_{\min} > 0$), if a robot receives infinite independent informative observations, standard Bayesian martingale convergence yields $B_r^n(\theta^*) \to 1$ almost surely. However, in finite-horizon robotic missions ($T = 80$ ticks), actual adversaries exhibit non-stationary strategies, dynamic switching (such as cyclic bottleneck patrol $\theta_4$), and corridor partial observability. We demote asymptotic posterior concentration from a mathematical guarantee to an empirical finding: while the Dirichlet filter adaptively concentrates belief weight toward active risk corridors within $15$--$25$ edge traversals, exact parameter convergence is neither achieved nor mathematically required for the safe-claim margin to function.

\begin{proposition}[Lipschitz Continuity of Expected Recourse Bids]
For any task $j \in \mathcal{T}$, let the pure-type task sensitivity be defined as $L_j = \Delta_j = \max_{\theta \in \Theta} c_r(j \mid \theta) - \min_{\theta \in \Theta} c_r(j \mid \theta)$. The expected recourse bid function $c_r(j \mid B) = \sum_{\theta} B(\theta) c_r(j \mid \theta)$ is $L_j$-Lipschitz continuous with respect to the Total Variation metric on belief space $\Delta(\Theta)$:
\begin{equation}
    |c_r(j \mid B) - c_r(j \mid B')| \leq L_j \cdot \mathrm{TV}(B, B')
\label{eq:bid_lipschitz}
\end{equation}
\label{prop:lipschitz}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $c_r(j \mid \theta) \in [\min_\theta c_r(j \mid \theta), \max_\theta c_r(j \mid \theta)]$ be bounded in an interval of length $\Delta_j$. By the Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality theorem for total variation distance:
\begin{align}
    |c_r(j \mid B) - c_r(j \mid B')| &= \left| \sum_{\theta \in \Theta} (B(\theta) - B'(\theta)) c_r(j \mid \theta) \right| \notag \\
    &\leq \Delta_j \cdot \mathrm{TV}(B, B')
\end{align}
which establishes Eq.~(\ref{eq:bid_lipschitz}) with Lipschitz constant $L_j = \Delta_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Conflict-Freedom of Phase-1 Safe Claims]
Let $L_j = \Delta_j = \max_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta) - \min_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta)$ denote the bid Lipschitz constant across adversary types. If Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} holds ($\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ for all $s \ne r$) and map discrepancy satisfies $|c_r(j) - c_s(j)| \leq \delta_{\text{map}}$, then two robots $r$ and $s$ evaluating the safe-claim rule in Eq.~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}) with Pinsker margin $m_{r, j}$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:pinsker_margin}) in the same allocation round \textbf{cannot simultaneously claim the same task $j$ in Phase~1}, even though their subjective beliefs are not synchronized.
\label{thm:conflict_freedom}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose for contradiction that both robot $r$ and robot $s$ simultaneously claim task $j$ via the safe-claim rule in Phase~1. By Eq.~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}):
\begin{align}
    \hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} &< \hat{c}_s^r(j) \label{eq:r_ineq} \\
    \hat{c}_s^s(j) + m_{s, j} &< \hat{c}_r^s(j) \label{eq:s_ineq}
\end{align}
Summing~(\ref{eq:r_ineq}) and~(\ref{eq:s_ineq}) yields:
\begin{equation}
    m_{r, j} + m_{s, j} < \left( \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j) \right) + \left( \hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j) \right)
\label{eq:sum_ineq}
\end{equation}
By the triangle inequality of total variation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) \leq \mathrm{TV}(B_r, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) + \mathrm{TV}(\hat{B}_s^{(r)}, B_s)
\end{equation}
Applying Pinsker's inequality to the first term and Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} to the second:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) \leq \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D_{\text{KL}}(B_r \parallel \hat{B}_s^{(r)})} + \eta_{\text{eff}}
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:lipschitz}, the variation in expected cost across subjective beliefs satisfies $|\hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j)| \leq L_j \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) + \delta_{\text{map}}$. Substituting the Pinsker upper bound yields:
\begin{equation}
    |\hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j)| \leq \Delta_j \left( \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}} \right) + \delta_{\text{map}} = m_{r, j}
\end{equation}
Similarly, by symmetry of total variation and the common sensitivity bound $\Delta_j$, $|\hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j)| \leq m_{s, j}$. Therefore:
\begin{equation}
    \left( \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j) \right) + \left( \hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j) \right) \leq m_{r, j} + m_{s, j}
\end{equation}
which contradicts the strict inequality in Eq.~(\ref{eq:sum_ineq}). Hence, simultaneous safe claims in Phase~1 cannot occur.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textit{Remark 1 (Hierarchical Adherence Levels of Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} and Empirical Verification of Theorem~1):}
Theorem~\ref{thm:conflict_freedom} is strictly conditional on Assumption~\ref{ass:tom}. Across our comprehensive benchmark, we measure empirical adherence to $\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s) \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ at three distinct hierarchical levels:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Per-Evaluation Adherence (\aeAdherencePerEval\%):} Fraction of total periodic belief evaluations across all mission timesteps and candidate tasks satisfying $\mathrm{TV} \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ (955,104 of 1,466,999 evaluations).
    \item \textbf{Per-Instance Adherence (\aeAdherencePerInstance\%):} Mean adherence rate across independent benchmark instances ($N = \aeMatchedBenchmarkInstances$ graph-seed instances; 95\% bootstrap CI: $[53.2\%, 58.9\%]$), strictly rejecting the idealized 90\% threshold ($p = 1.000$).
    \item \textbf{Per-Safe-Claim Adherence (\aeAdherencePerClaim\%):} Fraction of executed local safe claims where peer belief error satisfied $\mathrm{TV} \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$ at the specific timestep of claim commitment (\aeSafeClaimsAdhered\ of \aeSafeClaimsTotal\ safe claims). \textbf{This claim-level adherence is the exact antecedent required by Theorem~\ref{thm:conflict_freedom}.}
\end{enumerate}
\noindent Across all \aeSafeClaimsAdhered\ safe claims satisfying $\mathrm{TV} \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$, exactly \textbf{\aeTheoremConflicts\ double-claim conflicts occurred}, confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:conflict_freedom} empirically with an exact one-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson upper bound of \aeTheoremConflictRateUpper\% on the conflict rate. Broken down by allocation time step, empirical conflict-freedom holds across both early initial-round claims ($t = 0$: $n = \aeTheoremEarlyN$, 0 conflicts, 95\% upper bound \aeTheoremEarlyUpper\%) and late dynamic recourse claims ($t > 0$: $n = \aeTheoremLateN$, 0 conflicts, 95\% upper bound \aeTheoremLateUpper\%). Broken down by communication regime, conflict-freedom holds under nominal comms ($n = \aeTheoremNominalN$, 0 conflicts, upper bound \aeTheoremNominalUpper\%) as well as under degraded and stress-test perturbation regimes (range limits, packet loss, partitions, Poisson arrivals: $n = \aeTheoremPerturbN$, 0 conflicts, upper bound \aeTheoremPerturbUpper\%). Because Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} is an idealized condition holding for \aeAdherencePerClaim\% of safe claims, the evidence supporting safe-claim conflict-freedom is strictly \textbf{empirical}. When $\mathrm{TV} > \eta_{\text{eff}}$, ACA conservatively defers \aeHfourDeferralPct\% of tasks to Phase~2 consensus rounds, preserving an overall fleet-wide conflict-free-run rate of \aeConflictFreeRunPct\% (95\% CI: $[97.6\%, 100.0\%]$).

\begin{proposition}[Safe-Claim Monotonicity in Two-Phase Auctions]
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} and symmetric map bounds, any task $j$ claimed by robot $r$ in Phase~1 under condition~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}) is monotonically preserved in robot $r$'s bundle during Phase~2 consensus rounds without being revoked by peer bids.
\label{prop:safe_monotonicity}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Eq.~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}), task $j$ claimed in Phase~1 satisfies $\hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} < \min_{s \ne r} \hat{c}_s^r(j)$. For any peer $s \ne r$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:lipschitz} and the Pinsker triangle bound, peer $s$'s subjective valuation satisfies $\hat{c}_s^s(j) \ge \hat{c}_s^r(j) - m_{r, j} > \hat{c}_r^r(j)$. Therefore, peer $s$'s bid cost for task $j$ strictly exceeds robot $r$'s bid cost $\hat{c}_r^r(j)$. Under standard CBBA consensus rules (Action Y1/Y2)~\cite{choi2009cbba}, peer $s$ concedes the winning claim to robot $r$ upon receiving $r$'s winning bid value and timestamp, and cannot outbid $r$. Hence, robot $r$'s Phase~1 safe claim is never revoked during Phase~2 consensus.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Message Reduction via Local Recourse]
Let $q_j$ denote the probability that precomputed recourse detours up to depth $d$ are exhausted. Under independent edge blockages, $q_j \le \prod_{k=1}^d \pi_{(k)}$. Assuming an idealized setting where detours succeed with probability $1 - q_j$, the expected re-auction message overhead satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[\text{Msgs}_{\text{ReAuction}}] \le \sum_{j} q_j \mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{R}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(G))
\end{equation}
compared to $\sum_j [1 - \prod_i (1 - \pi_i)] \mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{R}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(G))$ when every individual corridor blockage triggers a full fleet-wide market re-auction.
\label{prop:messages}
\end{proposition}

\subsubsection{Gains over Standard CBBA}

Consequently, standard CBBA achieves only $\aeCbbaComp \pm \aeCbbaCompCI\%$ completion while exchanging $\aeCbbaMsgs \pm \aeCbbaMsgsCI$ messages. \subsubsection{Headline Comparison: ACA vs. CBBA+LR}
To isolate the benefit of ACA's market mechanism beyond raw detour execution, our primary headline comparison evaluates ACA against CBBA equipped with identical local recourse (+LR). As registered in $H_2$, ACA achieves a statistically significant completion gain of $+1.90$~percentage points over CBBA+LR (Hodges--Lehmann median difference $+1.90$~pp, 95\% CI $[+1.11, +3.02]$~pp, $p = \aePvalCbbaLr$). \subsubsection{Ablation Analysis: Recourse, Margin, and Inference}
To isolate the distinct mechanisms in ACA, we analyzed three clean ablations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Recourse Depth Ablation (ACA Depth-0, $H_1$):} Disabling contingency recourse ($d = 0$) while retaining the Pinsker margin and consensus drops task completion to $\aeAcaDepthZeroComp \pm \aeAcaDepthZeroCompCI\%$ (mean difference $+4.14$~pp, HL median $+3.33$~pp, $[+2.22, +4.44]$~pp, adjusted $p < 10^{-10}$) and increases message volume to $\aeAcaDepthZeroMsgs \pm \aeAcaDepthZeroMsgsCI$. This confirms that contingency recourse evaluation is the primary driver of completion under blockage.
    \item \textbf{Margin Ablation and Cost Decomposition (ACA No Margin, $H_4$):} Evaluating ACA without the Pinsker claim margin yields $\aeAcaNoMarginComp \pm \aeAcaNoMarginCompCI\%$ completion and $\aeAcaNoMarginTotConf \pm \aeAcaNoMarginTotConfCI$ total conflicts. In Phase~1, the Pinsker margin operates conservatively: under network latency, $\aeHfourDeferralPct\%$ of claims are deferred to Phase~2 consensus. Crucially, as predicted by Theorem~1, safe claims reduce safe-claim conflicts by $\aeHfourSafeConflictReductionPct\%$ relative to ACA No Margin (reducing conflicts from 3,646 to 32, Wilcoxon $p < 10^{-40}$). This conservative posture incurs a modest completion preservation cost of $\approx 1.5$ percentage points ($\aeAcaComp\%$ vs.\ $\aeAcaNoMarginComp\%$), which is confirmed non-inferior under Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) within a pre-registered 5.0 percentage point equivalence margin ($p < 10^{-18}$).
    \item \textbf{Bayesian Type Inference Parity (ACA No Inference, $H_5$):} Evaluating Bayesian adversary inference against a static uniform prior ablation (ACA No Inference) reveals statistical parity: task completion is $\aeAcaComp \pm \aeAcaCompCI\%$ for ACA vs.\ $\aeNoInfComp \pm \aeNoInfCompCI\%$ for ACA No Inference (difference $\aeHfiveCompDiff$ percentage points, HL median $0.00$~pp, adjusted $p = \aePvalInferenceNull$). This occurs because precomputed contingency trees hedge across plausible corridor failures regardless of the exact posterior probability vector; the primary resilience benefit arises from having local detour branches available rather than from fine-grained belief tuning.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
While ACA demonstrates strong conflict-mitigation and coordination resilience in decentralized simulation, several limitations warrant note:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Software Simulation Scope:} All empirical evaluations in this work are conducted in discrete graph simulations and continuous physics simulations in PyBullet. Remote multi-robot trials on physical robotic testbeds (such as the Robotarium testbed) remain planned future work; no physical robots were deployed in this study.
    \item \textbf{Estimation Error Bounds:} Theorem~1 guarantees conflict-free safe claiming provided $\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s) \leq \eta_{\text{eff}}$. Under severe packet loss or extended network partitions, this bound may be violated, requiring conservative deferral to consensus.
    \item \textbf{Contingency Tree Scalability:} Discrete recourse trees grow exponentially with depth $d$; practical deployments must employ depth pruning ($d \in \{1, 2\}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA) for decentralized multi-robot task allocation under strategic path blockages and inconsistent beliefs. By coupling type-aware expected recourse cost valuation with a closed-form Pinsker claim margin and autonomous local detour execution, ACA reduces communication overhead and mitigates allocation conflicts without continuous belief synchronization. Paired empirical evaluations across diverse graph topologies reveal nuanced comparative trade-offs: ACA significantly outperforms CBBA+LR ($+1.90$~pp, $p = \aePvalCbbaLr$), achieves approximate parity with CBBA-PR+LR ($+0.25$~pp, $p = \aePvalCbbaPrLr$), but is surpassed in raw completion by reactive single-agent replanning (CTP Replan, Implicit Coordination) which sidesteps consensus negotiation. ACA's primary operational strengths are its $\aeHfourSafeConflictReductionPct\%$ reduction in safe-claim conflicts through the claim margin, its $\aeConflictFreeRunPct\%$ conflict-free-run rate despite empirical total variation violations, and its zero-message local recourse autonomy.
