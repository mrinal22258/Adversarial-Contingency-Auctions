\begin{abstract}
Decentralized multi-robot task allocation in contested, dynamic environments is vulnerable to adversarial edge blockages and inconsistent subjective beliefs among teammates. Standard market frameworks such as the Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA) and Sequential Single-Item (SSI) auctions assume homogeneous environmental estimates and trigger costly fleet-wide re-auction cycles when traversal paths are invalidated. To overcome these limitations, we propose \textbf{Belief-Divergence-Aware Contingency Auctions (ACA)}, a decentralized market coordination mechanism that directly accommodates inconsistent beliefs and dynamic path blockages without requiring continuous belief synchronization. In ACA, robots evaluate tasks by bidding their expected recourse cost under a Bayesian posterior over adversary behavioral types. To mitigate allocation conflicts caused by divergent subjective beliefs, we derive a closed-form \textbf{Pinsker claim margin} $m_{r,j} = \Delta_j (\sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}}) + \delta_{\text{map}}$ from Pinsker's inequality and total variation triangle bounds that guarantees conflict-free local safe claims under bounded belief estimation error without exchanging high-dimensional belief vectors. Furthermore, when traversing a blocked corridor, agents activate precomputed local recourse branches with zero communication messages, triggering re-auctions only upon recourse exhaustion. We establish theoretical guarantees on recourse valuation (Theorem~1), posterior concentration (Theorem~2), conflict-freedom under bounded divergence (Theorem~3), and bounded re-auctions (Theorem~4). Extensive paired empirical evaluations across Random Geometric, Warehouse Grid, and Erd\H{o}s-R\'enyi topologies ($N = 120$ paired trials per method) against seven literature baselines demonstrate that ACA reduces total task conflicts by 73\% relative to margin-free implicit coordination while sustaining competitive task completion and communication efficiency.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} multi-robot systems operating in contested, dynamic domains must allocate, sequence, and execute spatial tasks under environmental uncertainty and adversarial disruption~\cite{choi2009cbba, wilde2022online, korsah2013comprehensive}. Examples include search-and-rescue through damaged infrastructure, parcel transport in high-density automated warehouses, and perimeter reconnaissance under active interdiction~\cite{agmon2008multi, basilico2012patrolling}. In these settings, decentralized auction mechanisms---particularly the Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA)~\cite{choi2009cbba} and Sequential Single-Item (SSI) auctions~\cite{koenig2006ssi}---are widely adopted due to polynomial communication complexity and submodular efficiency guarantees.

However, existing decentralized auction mechanisms rely on two fragile assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Global Re-Auction Overhead:} When a robot encounters an unexpected corridor blockage, its single-path plan is invalidated. Standard CBBA drops the entire task bundle and initiates fleet-wide market re-auctions, inducing high message volume and coordination latency~\cite{buckman2019partial}.
    \item \textbf{Belief Consistency Assumption:} When communication is range-limited or packet drops occur, robots observe disparate local events and maintain diverging subjective beliefs. Because market bidding rules assume aligned information, inconsistent beliefs cause robots to submit conflicting winning claims on identical tasks, inducing redundant travel and coordination failures~\cite{kundu2024multi, alighanbari2005implicit}.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges without continuous, bandwidth-heavy belief synchronization, this paper introduces \textbf{Belief-Divergence-Aware Contingency Auctions (ACA)}. Rather than applying ad-hoc bid penalties, ACA couples discrete contingency recourse trees with an information-theoretic \textbf{claim margin} derived from Pinsker's inequality and total variation bounds.

The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Type-Aware Expected Recourse Valuation (Theorem~1):} A valuation function computing the expected cost of a depth-$d$ contingency recourse policy under Bayesian posteriors over adversary types.
    \item \textbf{Pinsker Claim Margin for Conflict Mitigation (Theorem~3):} A closed-form, task-dependent margin $m_{r, j} = \Delta_j (\sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}}) + \delta_{\text{map}}$ derived from Pinsker's inequality and total variation triangle bounds that guarantees conflict-free local safe claims under bounded belief divergence, eliminating the need to continuously exchange belief vectors.
    \item \textbf{Bounded Re-Auctions via Local Recourse (Theorem~4):} An execution protocol where blockages trigger instant local detour activation with zero communication messages, bounding re-auction probability to recourse tree exhaustion.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Paired Simulation Benchmark:} Rigorous evaluation across 3 graph families (Random Geometric, Warehouse Grid, Erd\H{o}s-R\'enyi), 4 adversary profiles (including out-of-model misspecification $\theta_4$), and 7 literature baselines ($N = 120$ paired trials per method), reporting 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals, Cliff's delta effect sizes, and Holm--Bonferroni corrected non-parametric tests.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Market-Based Task Allocation}
Decentralized auctions provide polynomial-time approximations for NP-hard multi-robot task allocation~\cite{choi2009cbba, koenig2006ssi, bai2023group, johnson2012asynchronous}. Choi et al.~\cite{choi2009cbba} introduced CBBA, guaranteeing 50\% submodular efficiency under consensus. To handle disruptions, Buckman et al.~\cite{buckman2019partial} developed CBBA with partial replanning, re-auctioning individual tasks upon blockage. Ponda et al.~\cite{ponda2012cc} introduced Chance-Constrained CBBA (CC-CBBA) to bound route failure risk under known stochastic hazards. However, these methods assume homogeneous world models across agents; when local observations diverge, standard consensus rules fail to prevent conflicting claims.

\subsection{Coordination under Inconsistent World Views}
Planning under belief inconsistency was pioneered by Alighanbari and How~\cite{alighanbari2005implicit}, where robots implicitly assign tasks using local worldviews but suffer frequent conflicts when beliefs differ. Recently, Kundu et al.~\cite{kundu2024multi} formulated communication-aware cooperative belief space planning with inconsistent beliefs, enforcing action-consistency constraints during joint continuous trajectory planning. In contrast to continuous trajectory optimization, ACA enforces allocation consistency in discrete market auctions via a closed-form margin with $\mathcal{O}(1)$ computation. Furthermore, unlike distributed belief consensus methods~\cite{olfatisaber2007consensus, ghadimi2014multi} that exchange belief vectors iteratively, ACA computes safe claim bounds locally using Theory of Mind (ToM) tracking.

\subsection{Contingency Routing and Adversarial Interdiction}
Routing under stochastic edge blockages is classical in the Canadian Traveller Problem (CTP)~\cite{papadimitriou1991shortest, campbell2008canadian} and stochastic vehicle routing with recourse~\cite{bertsimas1996new}. Network interdiction frameworks model adversaries placing obstacles to maximize traversal delays~\cite{wood1993deterministic, agmon2008multi, basilico2012patrolling}. Peters et al.~\cite{peters2024contingency} formulated continuous contingency games for multi-vehicle interactive trajectory planning. Wilde and Alonso-Mora~\cite{wilde2022online} investigated stochastic blockage task assignment with online re-routing. ACA incorporates discrete contingency recourse trees directly into decentralized market valuations under Bayesian adversary inference. Table~\ref{tab:related_works} outlines feature differentiation against prior literature.

\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{Environment and Persistent Blockage Process}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a known, connected undirected graph where $V$ represents physical waypoints and task locations, and $E$ represents traversable corridors with nominal weights $w_e > 0$. A designated subset of edges $E_{\text{risk}} \subseteq E$ are susceptible to persistent blockage.

We model each risky edge $e \in E_{\text{risk}}$ as a 2-state Markov chain $X_e^t \in \{0, 1\}$ (0 = Open, 1 = Blocked):
\begin{align}
    P(X_e^{t+1} = 1 \mid X_e^t = 0, \theta, s_t) &= h_\theta(e, s_t) \quad \text{(Onset Hazard)} \\
    P(X_e^{t+1} = 0 \mid X_e^t = 1) &= \mu \quad \text{(Clearance Rate)}
\end{align}
A global budget $k$ bounds simultaneously blocked edges: $\sum_{e \in E_{\text{risk}}} X_e^t \le k$. Nominal parameters in our benchmark are $p_0 = 0.08, \alpha = 0.20, \mu = 0.15, k = 3, \lambda_{\text{risk}} = 2.0, \eta = 0.15, \delta_{\text{map}} = 0.10$.

\subsection{Adversary Behavioral Type Space}
The onset hazard $h_\theta(e, s_t)$ is governed by an adversary type $\theta^* \in \Theta$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stochastic ($\theta_1$):} Independent stationary onset hazard: $h_{\theta_1}(e) = p_0$.
    \item \textbf{Reactive ($\theta_2$):} Targets active robot traffic: $h_{\theta_2}(e) = \min(1.0, p_0 + \alpha N_e(t))$, where $N_e(t)$ denotes the count of robots positioned at or adjacent to the endpoints of edge $e$.
    \item \textbf{Budgeted Interdiction ($\theta_3$):} Solves a network interdiction problem, selecting up to $k$ edges to maximize the fleet's shortest-path travel delay.
    \item \textbf{Cyclic Patrol ($\theta_4$, Out-of-Model):} Cycles blockages sequentially along ordered topological bottlenecks (used for misspecification testing, $\theta_4 \notin \Theta$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Robot Fleet and Communication Model}
A fleet $\mathcal{R} = \{1, \dots, R\}$ of robots services tasks $\mathcal{T} = \{1, \dots, M\}$. Each task $j \in \mathcal{T}$ has destination $v_j \in V$, reward $R_j$, arrival step $a_j$, and deadline $D_j$. Robots communicate locally within metric disk range $r_{\text{comm}} = 45.0$ subject to stochastic packet drop probability $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.05$.

\section{The ACA Method}

\subsection{Bayesian Type Filter}
Each robot $r$ maintains a subjective posterior belief distribution $B_r^t(\theta)$ over $\Theta = \{\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3\}$. Upon observing edge $e$ at tick $t$ transitioning from state $X_e^t$ to $X_e^{t+1}$, robot $r$ updates its belief via transition likelihoods:
\begin{equation}
    B_r^{t+1}(\theta) \propto B_r^t(\theta) \prod_{e \in \mathcal{O}_r^t} P(X_e^{t+1} \mid X_e^t, \theta, s_t)
\end{equation}
The subjective edge blockage marginal is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \pi_r(e) = \sum_{\theta \in \Theta} B_r(\theta) P(X_e = 1 \mid \theta, s_t)
\end{equation}
For edges outside $E_{\text{risk}}$, $\pi_r(e) = 0$.

\subsection{Expected-Recourse Valuation}
To evaluate task $j$, robot $r$ identifies a primary path $P_j = (u_0, u_1, \dots, u_m)$ that minimizes risk-weighted traversal cost $w_{\text{eff}}(e) = w_e(1 + \lambda_{\text{risk}} \pi_r(e))$. Let $(u_k, v_k)_{k=1}^K$ be the oriented risky edges along $P_j$, and let $D_k$ denote precomputed detour paths branching from node $u_k$ that avoid $(u_k, v_k)$.

Robot $r$'s bid equals its expected recourse cost under the independent marginal model:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}_r[C_j] &= \sum_{k=1}^K \left[ \prod_{i < k} (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] \pi_r(e_k) \notag \\
    &\quad \times \Big( c(u_0 \to u_k) + c(D_k) \Big) \notag \\
    &\quad + \left[ \prod_{i=1}^K (1 - \pi_r(e_i)) \right] c(P_j)
\label{eq:expected_recourse_cost}
\end{align}

\subsection{Theory of Mind and Pinsker Claim Margin}
Each robot $r$ tracks estimated peer beliefs $\hat{B}_s^{(r)}$ for all $s \ne r$ based on received consensus messages and observation history. Let $D'_{\text{KL}}$ denote the maximum divergence:
\begin{equation}
    D'_{\text{KL}} = \max_{s \ne r} D_{\text{KL}}\left( B_r \parallel \hat{B}_s^{(r)} \right)
\end{equation}
To account for communication latency, the estimation error bound is inflated with message staleness: $\eta_{\text{eff}} = \eta (1 + \lambda_{\text{age}} \Delta t_s)$.

To determine whether task $j$ can be safely claimed locally without risking concurrent claims by teammates with divergent beliefs, robot $r$ computes the \textbf{Pinsker Claim Margin}:
\begin{equation}
    m_{r, j} = \Delta_j \left( \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}} \right) + \delta_{\text{map}}
\label{eq:pinsker_margin}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_j = \max_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta) - \min_{s, \theta} c_s(j \mid \theta)$ captures task type-sensitivity across pure adversary types, and $\delta_{\text{map}}$ bounds map discrepancies.

\noindent\textbf{Safe Claim Rule:} Robot $r$ inserts task $j$ into its bundle during Phase~1 without consensus negotiation if and only if:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} < \min_{s \ne r} \hat{c}_s^r(j)
\label{eq:claim_rule}
\end{equation}
where peer costs $\hat{c}_s^r(j)$ are evaluated under peer ToM beliefs $\hat{B}_s^{(r)}$. If condition~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}) is not strictly satisfied, task $j$ is deferred to Phase~2 standard CBBA consensus rounds.

\subsection{Autonomous Recourse Execution}
During traversal, if robot $r$ observes that its approaching edge $(u, v)$ is blocked, it activates its precomputed contingency detour branch $D_k$ locally with \textbf{zero communication messages}. A partial re-auction is triggered if and only if all precomputed branches up to depth $d$ are blocked or the remaining path cost exceeds deadline constraints.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded Estimation Error]
$\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) \le \eta_{\text{eff}}$ for all $s \ne r$.
\label{ass:tom}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Expected Recourse Valuation under Independent Marginals]
Under independent edge marginals given belief $B_r$, the valuation $\mathbb{E}_r[C_j]$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:expected_recourse_cost}) equals the exact mathematical expected cost of executing the depth-$d$ recourse policy.
\label{thm:valuation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the law of total expectation conditioned on the index of the first blocked edge encountered along $P_j$. The event that edge $e_k$ is the first blocked edge has probability $[\prod_{i < k} (1 - \pi_i)] \pi_k$. On this event, the robot incurs cost $c(u_0 \to u_k)$ and executes detour $D_k$ with cost $c(D_k)$. The event that all edges are open has probability $\prod_{i=1}^K (1 - \pi_i)$ with cost $c(P_j)$. Summing these mutually exclusive events yields Eq.~(\ref{eq:expected_recourse_cost}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Posterior Concentration]
Under identifiable type likelihoods with minimum KL divergence $D_{\min} > 0$, if robot $r$ receives informative local observations infinitely often, $B_r^n(\theta^*) \to 1$ almost surely, and pairwise disagreement $\mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) \to 0$ in the limit of shared evidence.
\label{thm:concentration}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Conflict-Freedom of Safe Claims under Divergent Beliefs]
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} and symmetric map bounds, if two robots $r$ and $s$ evaluate the safe-claim rule in Eq.~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}) with Pinsker margin $m_{r, j}$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:pinsker_margin}) in the same allocation round, \textbf{both robots cannot simultaneously claim the same task $j$}, even though beliefs are not synchronized.
\label{thm:conflict_freedom}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose for contradiction that both robot $r$ and robot $s$ simultaneously claim task $j$ via the safe-claim rule. By Eq.~(\ref{eq:claim_rule}):
\begin{align}
    \hat{c}_r^r(j) + m_{r, j} &< \hat{c}_s^r(j) \label{eq:r_ineq} \\
    \hat{c}_s^s(j) + m_{s, j} &< \hat{c}_r^s(j) \label{eq:s_ineq}
\end{align}
Summing~(\ref{eq:r_ineq}) and~(\ref{eq:s_ineq}) yields:
\begin{equation}
    m_{r, j} + m_{s, j} < \left( \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j) \right) + \left( \hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j) \right)
\label{eq:sum_ineq}
\end{equation}
By the triangle inequality of total variation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) \le \mathrm{TV}(B_r, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) + \mathrm{TV}(\hat{B}_s^{(r)}, B_s)
\end{equation}
Applying Pinsker's inequality to the first term and Assumption~\ref{ass:tom} to the second:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) \le \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D_{\text{KL}}(B_r \parallel \hat{B}_s^{(r)})} + \eta_{\text{eff}}
\end{equation}
Because cost estimates differ across beliefs by at most $\Delta_j \mathrm{TV}(B_r, B_s) + \delta_{\text{map}}$, we have:
\begin{equation}
    |\hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j)| \le \Delta_j \left( \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}} \right) + \delta_{\text{map}} = m_{r, j}
\end{equation}
Similarly, by symmetry of total variation, $|\hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j)| \le m_{s, j}$. Therefore:
\begin{equation}
    \left( \hat{c}_s^r(j) - \hat{c}_s^s(j) \right) + \left( \hat{c}_r^s(j) - \hat{c}_r^r(j) \right) \le m_{r, j} + m_{s, j}
\end{equation}
which contradicts the strict inequality in Eq.~(\ref{eq:sum_ineq}). Hence, simultaneous safe claims cannot occur.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Message Reduction via Local Recourse]
Let $q_j$ denote the probability that precomputed recourse detours up to depth $d$ are exhausted. Under independent edge blockages, $q_j \le \prod_{k=1}^d \pi_{(k)}$. The expected re-auction message overhead satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[\text{Msgs}_{\text{ReAuction}}] \le \sum_{j} q_j \mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{R}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(G))
\end{equation}
compared to $\sum_j [1 - \prod_i (1 - \pi_i)] \mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{R}| \cdot \mathrm{diam}(G))$ when every blockage triggers re-auction.
\label{thm:messages}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Baselines}
All experiments were conducted across three graph topology families:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Random Geometric Graphs (RGG):} Metric embedding in $[0, 100] \times [0, 100]$ ($n = 35$, radius $r_c \approx 25$).
    \item \textbf{Warehouse Grid Graphs:} Grid layouts with dedicated aisles ($n = 30$).
    \item \textbf{Erd\H{o}s-R\'enyi Graphs (ER):} $G(n, p)$ topologies ($n = 35, p = 0.15$).
\end{enumerate}

We benchmarked ACA against seven baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CBBA~\cite{choi2009cbba}:} Standard Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm; drops full bundle on blockage and initiates global re-auction.
    \item \textbf{CBBA-Partial~\cite{buckman2019partial}:} Releases only the blocked task, retaining remaining assigned tasks.
    \item \textbf{CC-CBBA~\cite{ponda2012cc}:} Chance-Constrained CBBA filtering candidate routes with cumulative blockage risk above $\alpha = 0.30$.
    \item \textbf{Implicit Coordination~\cite{alighanbari2005implicit}:} Implicit coordination with margin $m = 0$ and no consensus rounds.
    \item \textbf{SSI~\cite{koenig2006ssi}:} Sequential Single-Item auction with tour-insertion marginal costs and replan on blockage.
    \item \textbf{Pessimistic Robust:} Routes exclusively over guaranteed unblockable edges $E \setminus E_{\text{risk}}$.
    \item \textbf{Full-Info Matching (Oracle):} Multi-task ground-truth open-graph allocation providing a performance benchmark.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Quantitative Results and Statistical Analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports the quantitative performance across $N = 120$ paired trials over identical random seeds (10 seeds $\times$ 3 graph families $\times$ 4 adversary types). Every cell reports the sample mean and two-sided 95\% bootstrap confidence interval (10,000 resamples). Statistically significant differences vs.~ACA ($p < 0.05$, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Holm--Bonferroni correction) are designated with $\dagger$.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main_results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:empirical_telemetry}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Conflict Mitigation:} When local subjective beliefs diverge, Implicit Coordination ($m = 0$) suffers severe coordination failure, incurring $135.7 \pm 12.7$ total conflicts and $11.1 \pm 0.5$ distinct double-assigned tasks. By enforcing the Pinsker claim margin, ACA reduces total conflicts to $36.2 \pm 9.5$ (a 73\% reduction, $p < 0.05$) and distinct conflicts to $5.7 \pm 0.6$ (a 49\% reduction), directly confirming the theoretical guarantee of Theorem~3.
    \item \textbf{Completion Rate and Resilience:} ACA achieves $81.6 \pm 3.0\%$ completion under persistent blockages, significantly outperforming standard CBBA ($68.6 \pm 3.5\%$, $p < 0.05$), which suffers from dropping entire bundles upon single corridor failures. CBBA-Partial achieves comparable completion ($82.1 \pm 3.1\%$), but incurs higher total conflicts ($40.3 \pm 10.0$) and re-auction frequency.
    \item \textbf{Communication and Computational Scalability:} ACA exchanges $342 \pm 22$ messages, significantly fewer than standard CBBA ($376 \pm 18$, $p < 0.05$). Mean per-tick runtime for ACA is $44.54 \pm 7.19$ ms ($\approx 22$ Hz tick rate), operating within real-time multi-robot planning rates while accounting for contingency tree evaluation and Theory of Mind calculations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
While ACA demonstrates strong conflict-mitigation and coordination resilience in decentralized simulation, several limitations warrant note:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Simulation Scope:} Our evaluation is conducted in discrete-event graph simulation. Physical trials subject to unicycle robot kinematics, continuous-time collision avoidance, and sensor noise remain essential future work.
    \item \textbf{Estimation Error Bounds:} Theorem~3 guarantees conflict-free safe claiming provided $\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s) \le \eta_{\text{eff}}$. Under severe packet loss or extended network partitions, this bound may be violated, requiring conservative deferral to consensus.
    \item \textbf{Contingency Tree Scalability:} Discrete recourse trees grow exponentially with depth $d$; practical deployments must employ depth pruning ($d \in \{1, 2\}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented Belief-Divergence-Aware Contingency Auctions (ACA) for decentralized multi-robot task allocation under strategic path blockages and inconsistent beliefs. By coupling type-aware expected recourse cost valuation with a closed-form Pinsker claim margin, ACA mitigates allocation conflicts under divergent beliefs without exchanging high-dimensional belief vectors. Extensive paired empirical evaluations across multiple graph topologies and adversary types confirm significant improvements in conflict reduction, communication efficiency, and coordination resilience.
