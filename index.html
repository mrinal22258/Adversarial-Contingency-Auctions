TL;DR

Standard multi-robot auction mechanisms (CBBA, SSI) suffer cascading coordination resets when paths are maliciously blocked in adversarial environments. Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA) equips heterogeneous robot fleets with Bayesian belief modeling over hidden adversary policies ($\mathcal{P}(\theta^*)$), information-theoretic KL-divergence bid regularization, and precomputed contingency branching trees — enabling autonomous, zero-overhead localized detour activation without triggering expensive global market re-auctions.

Abstract

Decentralized multi-robot task allocation in contested, uncertain environments is vulnerable to strategic adversarial edge blockages and inconsistent local beliefs among teammates. Standard market frameworks such as the Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA) and Sequential Single-Item (SSI) auctions require fleet-wide consensus and trigger cascading re-auction cycles upon path invalidation, inducing severe communication spikes and coordination latency. To overcome these limitations, we propose Adversarial Contingency Auctions (ACA), a decentralized market coordination mechanism that directly addresses inconsistent beliefs and dynamic blockages without requiring continuous belief synchronization. In ACA, robots evaluate tasks by bidding their expected recourse cost under a Bayesian posterior over adversary types. To mitigate allocation conflicts when subjective beliefs diverge, we derive a closed-form Pinsker claim margin $m_{r,j} = \Delta_j (\sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D'_{\text{KL}}} + \eta_{\text{eff}}) + \delta_{\text{map}}$ from Pinsker's inequality and total variation triangle bounds that guarantees conflict-free safe allocation under bounded estimation error (Theorem 1, conditional on Assumption 1: $\mathrm{TV}(B_s, \hat{B}_s^{(r)}) \le \eta_{\text{eff}}$) without exchanging belief vectors. Furthermore, when traversing a blocked corridor, agents execute precomputed local recourse branches with zero communication messages, triggering re-auctions only upon recourse exhaustion. We establish theoretical properties for recourse valuation (Proposition 1), Lipschitz bid continuity (Proposition 2), conditional conflict-freedom (Theorem 1), and bounded messaging (Proposition 3). Extensive paired empirical evaluations across 5 graph topologies ($N = 13{,}800$ total sweep trials, including a balanced core grid of $N = 750$ paired trials per method across 250 independent instances) and a held-out confirmatory evaluation on seeds 101–130 ($N = 450$ instances) demonstrate that ACA's primary operational advantage is safe decentralized coordination via the Pinsker margin under communication constraints rather than unconstrained completion dominance.
